## 摘要正文

Been turning that Paul Graham phrase over since I was a kid reading essays off an old CRT monitor, and it took way longer than expected to actually get what make something people want means. Especially once you're the one actually shipping software instead of just reading about it.  First phase for me was just building the software. Felt good, but it wasn't enough. A tech news site two friends and I built, where I spent a genuinely embarrassing amount of time on the design, made zero dollars in revenue and we shut it down after a few weeks. We never once checked if enough people actually wanted to read another tech news site. We shipped what we thought was cool, not what anyone was paying for.  The real unlock was almost the opposite of building, just shut up and listen. Spent months talking to founders in SF with no agenda, not pitching anything, just asking what tools they were using and where those tools failed them. Eventually patterns show up in what people actually want versus what you assume they want, and you start acquiring a taste for it.
