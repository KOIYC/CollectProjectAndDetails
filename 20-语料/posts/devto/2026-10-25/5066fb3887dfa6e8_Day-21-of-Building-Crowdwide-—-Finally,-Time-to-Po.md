## 正文

I was building Crowdwide, a social media platform I started for new indie devs, smaller communities, and smaller creators to raise their voices higher.

Your voice shouldn't be judged based on how big a platform you join. Everyone's voice should be equal.

And today is **Day 21 of building Crowdwide!!!**

YESSSSS!! 🔥🔥

Crowdwide is in a mostly stable state now! Its core features, main algorithm, mailing system, and recommendation system, Widewave, are finally DONE!

So now, it's time to polish the UI.

On Day 21, I'm just polishing the UI of Crowdwide.

The things I thought needed the most improvement were the background color combination, theme adaptability, and sidebar (THIS NEEDS HEAVY IMPROVEMENT!).

The background used to have gradients, but when users scrolled a lot, the background stretched and created a weird shade at the top.

In some themes, a few text colors weren't looking good and were making things difficult to read.

And the sidebar... I was SO confused about how it should look!

Like, should it be on the left or right?

When it opens, should it compress the page?

Or should it stay over the page without compressing anything, making the rest of the screen a bit darker to keep the focus on it?

SO MANY QUESTIONS 😭

> **Odinaka Robert Nnamani** · 2026-10-23T17:21:42Z　
> The sequencing here is the thing I would highlight back to you: core algorithm and mailing system done before UI polish is exactly the order that keeps an MVP alive. I have seen the reverse too many times — founders polishing gradients while the activation path is broken, and the pretty UI becomes expensive camouflage for a product nobody gets to use. Making the mailing system part of the core feature set is the interesting call to me; most social MVPs defer notifications and then wonder why nobody comes back for a second visit. And overlaying the sidebar instead of compressing the page is the right instinct — reflow shifts on every toggle would have been a paper-cut complaint from every user, every session. One concrete question: for the theme contrast issues you fixed, are you checking those ratios by eye, or against a standard like WCAG? A systematic pass with a contrast checker before the next theme update would catch the bad pairs before your users do.

> **Odinaka Robert Nnamani** · 2026-10-24T07:49:00Z　
> It's mostly the second thing — the check measures math your eyes are bad at. The ratio comes from relative luminance with weights tuned to human vision (green counts far more than blue), and your eyes do local adaptation: mid-grey looks brighter against a dark background than the formula says it is. Thin strokes and large text cheat it further — they read fine at 20px even at 2.1:1, and the ratio treats all text the same regardless of size. So 'looks fine' is your brain's renderer and the ratio is the spec. Trust the spec — the projector in the demo room won't do local adaptation for you. Which page surprised you most when you ran axe?
