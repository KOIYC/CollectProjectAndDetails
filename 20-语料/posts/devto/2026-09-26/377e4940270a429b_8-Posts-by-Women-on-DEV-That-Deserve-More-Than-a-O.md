## 正文

We went looking for good posts by women on DEV. Not "inspirational," not "diverse" but just genuinely good, the kind you'd send to a friend with "okay you have to read this one" attached. Here are eight, and the honest reasons each one earns your attention.

> **A note from Mika, SheShips creator**
>
> This is the first post from SheShips, soooo hi. I'm Mika, and I started this because I kept finding great posts by women on DEV that and I wanted to bring them the attention they deserved, and I didn't see a space built specifically to change that.

## Anna Villarreal — DEV Follower Analysis

{% link https://dev.to/annavi11arrea1/dev-follower-analysis-4dhc %}

This is what a good tangent looks like. Anna was seven hours into an unrelated Node.js tutorial when the thought hit her sideways: what if she scraped her own DEV followers, pulled their public GitHub data via GraphQL, and actually looked at who was reading her stuff?

There wasn't a plug-and-play tool for this. So she built one — a scraper, a JSON export, a chart at the end. What makes the post worth reading isn't the follower-count gimmick, it's that she's honest about the process being genuinely a little annoying to figure out. "There isn't a plug-and-play solution for this, you have to be a tiny bit creative," she writes. That's the whole game, most days.

## sizzlebop — I Built a Local AI Music Studio

{% link https://dev.to/sizzlebop/i-built-a-local-ai-music-studio-3fb9 %}

A working answer to "why do I need a subscription and a cloud account just to mess around with AI music generation." Local-first tools like this get way less airtime than their SaaS equivalents, which is exactly why this one deserves the spotlight — someone did the less-flashy, more-useful version and wrote it up.

## sylwia-lask — What If Your AI Agent Never Had to Leave the Browser? (Demo)

{% link https://dev.to/sylwia-lask/what-if-your-ai-agent-never-had-to-leave-the-browser-demo--5g %}

Good because it's a demo, not a hot take. Half the AI-agent discourse right now is pure speculation in a thread somewhere. This one just answers the question by shipping the thing and letting you go look at it yourself.

## thesnehamk — MCP vs API Explained: Do We Still Need APIs After MCP?

{% link http://dev.to/thesnehamk/mcp-vs-api-explained-do-we-still-need-apis-after-mcp-2kkk %}

The explainer post that earns its spot by asking the question a lot of developers are quietly unsure about but don't want to say out loud in a meeting. Direct, timely, no padding.

## maame-codes — Disappeared Since March: Taking a Long Break Was My Best Decision Yet

{% link https://dev.to/maame-codes/disappeared-since-march-taking-a-long-break-was-my-best-decision-yet-1n45 %}

Not a build post, and that's exactly why it belongs on this list. Writing honestly about stepping away is harder than writing a project devlog, and rarer. If you've ever felt weird about a gap in your own output, this one's for you.

## Ava Barron — Can You Stand the Rain

{% link https://dev.to/abarron/can-you-stand-the-rain-4e0k %}

An origin story that refuses the tidy version. Her path into tech runs through an Alicia Keys concert and fixing friends' computers over AIM as a teenager — not a bootcamp, not a CS degree, not a straight line. It won DEV's own 2026 WeCoded "Echoes of Experience" category, and it earns that win through specificity — nothing in it is generic.

## Margarita — It Wasn't Brave. It Was Just Tuesday.

{% link https://dev.to/it_is_margarita/it-wasnt-brave-it-was-just-tuesday-3673 %}

Also a WeCoded winner, and what makes this one work is its refusal to dramatize. Three generations of one family, all pulled toward logic and systems, told plainly — no inflating it into a bigger moment than it actually was. The title does the whole argument by itself.

## Ashley Childress — Forged Between Coal and Code

{% link https://dev.to/anchildress1/forged-between-coal-and-code-phi %}

The most technically ambitious thing on this list. A fully narrated, self-voiced web experience about growing up in an Appalachian coal town and finding her way into engineering — backed by 685 unit tests and 220 E2E tests, for what's nominally a personal art piece. It won DEV's Frontend Art category, and honestly, the craftsmanship is doing as much work as the story is.

---

That's the list. Eight different reasons to click through, eight different kinds of good. Go read one.

### Building something? We want to see it.

SheShips is a space for women who build and ship real software — whatever that looks like for you. A devlog, a first open-source contribution, an experiment that didn't work, a tutorial explaining the thing you learned three days ago. If you've got something you're working on, [join us on DEV](https://dev.to/sheships) and share it. We'd love to feature you in the next one of these.
