## The Doors Are Open

A little over a month ago, I published this:

{% embed https://dev.to/georgekobaidze/show-me-the-article-youre-most-proud-of-and-ill-read-every-single-one-of-them-1l53 %}

The deal was simple. You pick the one article you're most proud of, not the one with the most reactions, the one you *love* the most. I read it and give you my honest feedback.

I expected maybe 15 or 20 replies. The thread ended up with over 250 comments.

Was it tough? Absolutely. There were evenings when my eyes desperately asked me to stop. But I read every single one.

That post had a second promise hidden at the very end: **"Let's build a hall of fame of DEV's best work!"**

So here it is.

Every article below was chosen by the person who wrote it. Not by an algorithm, not by a reactions count, not by me. By the author.

> 📌 **A quick note:** A few of you shared articles hosted outside DEV. I read them, but I haven't included them here. This hall only features articles published on DEV, because I can't vouch for external links, and I don't want to send readers somewhere I can't verify is safe.

---

## Visitor Stats 📊

Before the tour starts, a few numbers:

- **65 authors, 65 masterpieces.**
- **600+ minutes** of listed reading time. That's over 10 hours, and in reality it was much more, because listed reading time is a polite lie.
- **The longest read:** 68 minutes. It took me closer to 100.
- **The shortest read:** 1 minute.
- **The oldest exhibit:** published in March 2020.
- **The most reactions:** 623.
- **Zero reactions:** 8 articles had none when I collected them. Zero! That's exactly why this hall exists.

---

## How to Tour the Hall

The exhibits are grouped into wings by theme, so you can head straight to whatever interests you most. Each exhibit has the article itself, why the author picked it (when they told me), and my take.

- 🚪 **The Welcome Desk:** where every visitor should start
- 🏛️ **Stories From the Trenches:** careers, first steps, and the people behind the code
- 💡 **The "Finally, It Clicked" Gallery:** fundamentals explained the way they should be
- ⚙️ **The Engine Room:** systems, internals, and seriously deep dives
- 🤖 **The AI Lab:** building with AI, and finding out where it breaks
- 🧠 **The Philosophy Floor:** thinking about AI, engineering, and the craft itself
- 🔧 **The Builder's Garage:** real tools solving real problems

Grab a coffee. It's a big building.

## What I Learned From Reading All of This

**People pick their firsts.** A surprising number of you chose your very first article. Not your most polished one, not your most popular one, your first. The one you almost didn't publish. I get it, I feel the same way about mine.

**Reactions don't measure value.** Eight of these articles had zero reactions when I collected them, and several of you said yours "didn't get much interaction." Some of those were among the best things I read all month. The algorithm rewards timing, audience, and luck. It doesn't reward effort, and it doesn't reward what matters to the author.

**AI is everywhere, but the best AI posts are about where it breaks.** Rollback buttons, prompt injection tests that pass while the attack works, RAG thresholds that do nothing. The most valuable writing wasn't "look what AI can do", it was "here's what happened when I tested it properly."

**The range of this community is absurd.** Operating systems, ESP32 boards, databases built from scratch, evacuation routing on a Greek island, and a Christmas-themed legacy Java horror story. All in one thread.
