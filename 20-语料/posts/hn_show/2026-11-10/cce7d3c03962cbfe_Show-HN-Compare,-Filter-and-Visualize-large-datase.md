Built for Modern Data Professionals
 Our aim is to provide a no-nonsense alternative for individuals and smaller teams who are serious about data quality. All our tools are fully self-service, provided at a fair flat price, with no lock-in, no demos, no sales calls...
 Data Exploration
 Slicelytics provides tools to understand your JSON, NDJSON/JSONL, CSV, TOON, and FIT datasets. Our powerful filtering takes you way beyond what a generic json formatter can do.
 Our engine automatically infers schemas and maps data types across deeply nested structures of arbitrary depth. Filter by property name or by value (e.g. status="error" or level!="debug" ), with autocomplete for nested properties and every active filter shown as a chip you can remove in one click.
 Dealing with high-volume or compressed data? We natively support Gzip and Zstandard decompression right in your browser, ensuring your workflow remains local and private.

Structural Diffing at Scale
 Standard diff tools fail on rich, structured data. Our comparison engine is built for hierarchy, allowing you to track changes across multiple dataset versions side-by-side.
 Isolate deltas with intelligent filters that automatically adapt to your data's schema. Identify modifications in even the largest arrays and nested objects effortlessly. Both datasets can be large NDJSON files, streamed and loaded side by side, batch by batch.
 See the evolution of your state: track both original and updated values for every property and array item with clear, high-contrast highlighting. Share a diff as a single link that carries both datasets.

Zero-Config Data Visualization
 Transform tabular and nested data into compelling stories with no code required. Bridge the gap between raw logs and actionable intelligence.
 Calculate critical statistical metrics—mean, median, mode, standard deviation, variance, and more—and plot them instantly. Numbers and ISO 8601 dates are both supported.
 Choose from a comprehensive suite of performance-optimized visualizations: bar charts, line charts, scatter plots, histograms, bubble charts, radar maps, and beyond. Only your data sets the limit.

Share Data with a Link
 Send a colleague exactly what you see. Click Share to copy a link that opens the same page, properties, filters, and chart, with the data as you filtered it. On Compare, one link carries both datasets. They don't need an account to open it.
 The data is compressed into the part of the link after # , which browsers never send to a server. Nothing is uploaded, and nothing is stored by us.
 Links suit small and medium datasets, as browsers limit how long a link can be (about 2 MB in Chrome). Hide what your colleague doesn't need before sharing, and the link gets shorter.

See the full changelog Frequently Asked Questions
 Who is Slicelytics for? Slicelytics is built for software engineers, data scientists, and analysts who need to inspect and debug structured data locally. It is a self-service utility tool for individuals and small teams.
What use cases is Slicelytics built for? The core features are data exploration (filtering and traversing nested structures), structural diffing (comparing hierarchical data to identify additions, modifications, and deletions), and data visualization (generating charts and calculating statistical metrics without writing code).
What file formats are supported? Slicelytics parses JSON, NDJSON/JSONL, CSV, TSV, and TOON files, and FIT files from Garmin, Polar, Suunto and other fitness devices, plain or compressed with Gzip (.gz) or Zstandard (.zst).
Does Slicelytics work with compressed data? Yes. It includes native Gzip and Zstandard decompression implemented in the browser. Data is decompressed client-side without being sent to an external server.
What limitations are there? Since data processing happens entirely in the browser, performance is constrained by your local machine's memory capacity and the JavaScript engine's limits. Large NDJSON files are streamed in the background, in Explore and on both sides of Compare, with experimental support for files of several gigabytes. Files in other formats are read into memory whole, then shown 100 items at a time. Shared links are compressed, but browsers limit how long a link can be (about 2 MB in Chrome), so sharing suits small and medium datasets. Watching a folder and installing Slicelytics as an app need Chrome or Edge.
Can I use Slicelytics to collaborate with my colleagues? Yes, for small and medium datasets. Click the Share button in the bottom right corner to copy a link to what is on your screen: the data as filtered, plus the view (page, properties, filters, and chart settings). On Compare, the link carries both datasets. The data is compressed into the part of the link after # , which browsers never send to a server, so it is never stored by us. Your colleagues don't need an account or a subscription to open the link.
Can I filter JSON data by a value? Yes. Type a value filter such as status="error" in the properties filter to keep only the items where that property has that value, or use != to leave them out instead: level!="debug" keeps everything else, items without a level included. Filters work on nested properties too: records.type="sleep" keeps the matching items and also removes the non-matching elements from the nested records array.
Can AI agents like Claude Code open data in Slicelytics? Yes. An agent builds a Slicelytics link as described in slicelytics.com/llms.txt . The link opens Explore, Compare, or Visualize with your dataset and a ready-made view: which properties to show, value filters, the x-axis, and the chart type and metric. The data travels in the part of the link after # , so it is never sent to a server.
Can Slicelytics show new files as a script writes them? Yes, in Chrome and Edge. Click "Watch a folder" in the Explore view and pick a folder on your computer. Slicelytics lists its JSON, NDJSON, CSV, TOON, and FIT files, and new files show up in the list as they are written; click one to load it. The folder is remembered across visits.
Can I install Slicelytics as a desktop app? Yes, from Chrome or Edge. Installed, Slicelytics runs in its own window. Links you open go to the window you already have open, which offers them with a "Show" button instead of replacing what you are looking at.
What visualisation options are available? Available chart types include bar, line, pie, doughnut, scatter, bubble, radar, and polar area charts, plus histograms of how often each value occurs. Our Visualize tool also computes standard statistical metrics such as sum, mean, median, mode, min, max, range, standard deviation, and variance from (parts of) the loaded data. ISO 8601 dates and date-times can be charted too, and nested arrays such as matrices are charted as one series per row, or narrowed to the rows you pick, e.g. mlModel.layers.weights[0] .
