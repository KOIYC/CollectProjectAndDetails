More than a bracket maker
 Every event starts with a tournament bracket. Here is what the bracket maker hands you the moment your draw is live — and why organizers keep the whole event in the same workspace. No spreadsheets to chase, no group chats to moderate — the page itself does the updating.
Live brackets, not snapshots
 Rollare runs the bracket the way a real event does: record scores as games finish and the bracket maker advances winners on its own — byes, bracket resets and third-place matches included. Every player gets a link with the schedule, bracket and standings, so nobody texts you for updates. Recording a score takes seconds — on a phone at courtside or a laptop at the desk.
Free where it matters
 The bracket maker is free for 2 to 128 teams: unlimited brackets, no watermark, no expiry date. Paid plans only add custom branding on exports and embeds, plus multi-event room for organizers who run at scale — everything you generate today stays free. No credit card, no trial timer.

Tournament formats for every competition
 Every bracket type from casual rounds to championship play, with automatic seeding, byes and advancement. Each format is a full workflow — standings, tie-breaking rules and advancement logic included — and every card opens its own free generator.
Single Elimination
 Lose once and you're out. The classic knockout bracket — fast to run, easy to follow, with an optional third-place match for the bronze. Best for one-day events and large fields.
Double Elimination
 Two losses to be eliminated, with winners and losers brackets meeting in a grand final — full bracket-reset support lets a finalist force a decider.
Round Robin
 Everyone plays everyone, with a full standings table, head-to-head tie-breakers, and a double round robin option for home-and-away leagues.
Group Stage + Knockout
 Round robin in groups, then the top teams advance to a knockout bracket — qualifiers carry their seeds into the elimination rounds.
Swiss
 Each round pairs teams with similar records; no elimination, ranked by points. The standard for chess, card games and esports leagues. Fixed round counts make scheduling predictable.
Swiss + Knockout
 Swiss rounds first to cut the field, then the top-ranked teams advance to a knockout bracket that decides the title. The modern default for esports leagues and circuits.
