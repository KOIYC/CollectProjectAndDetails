 Three moments, and you know Wimi.
 Slots stay put. Windows move in and out of them, and none of them get closed.
 1
 Choose a layout
 Rest the mouse at the screen edge and the drawer slides in. Click a layout and the windows on that display move into its slots. No keys to learn, no config file.
2
 Open another window
 It takes the slot you’re working in, at the same size. Slots never get split smaller. The window it replaced is parked: still open, just out of the way.
3
 Bring a parked window back
 Click it in the drawer, where every window has a live preview, or switch to it with ⌘Tab or ⌥Tab as usual. It comes back into your slot.
The drawer
 See every window. Put it where you want it.
 Pick by preview Every open window, parked or not, with a live preview. Click one to bring it into your slot.
 Drag into a slot Drag a window from the drawer onto the slot you want it in.
 A layout for each display The drawer opens on the display you’re using, with that display’s layouts.
 Shortcuts if you want them Move, swap and rotate windows from the keyboard. Nothing requires it.
The layout picker: click one and the display follows. Halves, with the drawer open: the next window is one click away.
 The whole flow in one minute, recorded on a real Mac. Some parts are sped up. Questions
 What people asked at launch.
 Where did my other windows go?
 They’re parked: still open, tucked into a corner of the display. The drawer, ⌥Tab and search show them. Quitting Wimi puts every parked window back on screen.
 Does ⌘Tab bring them back?
 Yes. Switch to a parked window with ⌘Tab, ⌥Tab or the drawer and it takes the slot you’re in.
 Can I leave a window outside the layout?
 Yes. Pop a window out and it floats freely above the layout until you put it back.
 What about more than one display?
 Each display gets its own layout, and the drawer opens on whichever display you’re using.
