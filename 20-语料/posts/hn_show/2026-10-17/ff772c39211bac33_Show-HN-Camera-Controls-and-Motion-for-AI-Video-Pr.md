# AI Camera Controls

Where the camera sits, how much it sees and how it moves decide whether an AI clip feels directed or random. Below: interactive references for angle, movement and shot size, plus the vocabulary video models respond to. Every prompt copies with one click.

1. 1 Bind the reference
2. 2 Describe the scene
3. 3 Keep the look consistent
4. 4 Direct the camera

## Angle

Where the camera sits relative to the subject's eyeline. Same person, six heights.

Prompt Copy Eye-level shot. Camera: positioned at the subject's eye height, lens level with no tilt up or down. Framing: natural proportions, the horizon line crossing behind the subject near their eyes. Effect: neutral and honest; the viewer meets the subject as an equal. End: holds a level frame with no camera drift. Eye level — Neutral

## Movement

What the camera physically does during the shot. Watch how near and far layers separate.

### Pan right Pan / Tilt

Pan right. Camera: fixed on a tripod, rotates horizontally from left to right at an even speed. Framing: the scene slides across the frame while the horizon stays level. Effect: surveys the space and leads the eye. End: stops on the subject, framed on the right third. Click to copy

### Tilt up Pan / Tilt

Tilt up. Camera: fixed in place, pivots vertically upward from the ground. Framing: starts low on the ground and rises to the subject and the sky above. Effect: a reveal that builds scale. End: holds with the horizon in the lower third. Click to copy

### Zoom in Zoom / Lens

Slow zoom in. Camera: stays in the same position; only the lens magnification changes, no dolly. Framing: everything enlarges equally, with no parallax between foreground and background. Effect: scrutiny, as if someone is watching. End: holds on a tighter frame of the subject. Click to copy

### Rack focus Zoom / Lens

Rack focus. Camera: locked-off, no movement. Framing: focus begins on the foreground object and shifts smoothly to the subject behind it as the foreground melts into blur. Effect: redirects attention. End: holds sharp focus on the subject. Click to copy

### Dolly in Dolly / Track

Dolly in. Camera: moves forward on a straight track toward the subject, smooth and steady. Framing: the subject grows from a wide shot to a medium shot while foreground objects slide out of frame. Effect: rising intimacy and focus. End: settles on the medium shot and holds. Click to copy

### Dolly out Dolly / Track

Dolly out. Camera: moves backward on a straight track away from the subject, smooth and steady. Framing: the subject shrinks as more of the environment enters the frame. Effect: isolation or release. End: holds on a wide shot. Click to copy

### Truck left Dolly / Track

Truck left. Camera: slides sideways to the left, parallel to the subject, height and angle unchanged. Framing: near objects pass quickly while distant ones barely move. Effect: parallax and depth; a lateral reveal. End: stops with the subject on the right third. Click to copy

### Clockwise orbit Physical

Clockwise orbit. Camera: circles the subject at a fixed radius, smooth and continuous, travelling clockwise. Framing: the subject pinned centre while the whole world rotates behind them. Effect: gravity and importance; a moment crowned. End: completes its arc with the subject still centred. Click to copy

### Crane up Drone / Crane

Crane up. Camera: rises vertically on a crane arm, tilting down gently to keep the subject in frame. Framing: the ground falls away and the horizon drops as the camera climbs. Effect: release and scale; an ending. End: holds high above the subject. Click to copy

### Handheld Human Camera

Handheld. Camera: operated by hand, with natural micro-shake, breathing and small reframing corrections. Framing: loosely centred on the subject, never perfectly still. Effect: urgency and documentary realism. End: settles but never locks off. Click to copy

### Dolly zoom Specials

Dolly zoom. Camera: dollies backward while the lens zooms in at the same rate. Framing: the subject stays the same size while the background stretches and looms behind them. Effect: vertigo; the ground shifting under them. End: holds on the warped perspective. Click to copy

### Whip pan Specials

Whip pan. Camera: fixed on a tripod, snaps horizontally so fast the frame smears with motion blur. Framing: leaves one view and lands abruptly on the next. Effect: energy; a transition hidden inside a move. End: lands cleanly and holds still. Click to copy
