 How things work
Controls
Arrows / WASD Walk — hold to keep walking, tap for a single step
 Q E Z C Walk diagonally
 Numpad Walk in all eight directions
 Shift+direction Turn to face that way without stepping
 Drag a finger On a touch screen, double tap and drag — tap, lift, and put the finger straight back down — and you walk the way it points for as long as it stays down. Or hold a finger down until a ring comes up under it, then drag : the ring is a joystick. Two fingers dragged together walk too; spread or pinch them to zoom. A plain tap is a click, and one finger dragged without the double tap moves the view
 Click ground Walk there, on the floor you are on. Click or tap a stair, hole or ladder to go through it, even down or up to a floor you have never seen. Something lying under you or next to you is picked up instead. If there is no way there you are told so and do not move
 Click monster Attack it: it becomes your target, outlined in red, and you chase it (unless Chase is off). Click it again to let it go. A target that goes out of sight stays picked for a few seconds, then is let go
 Click player Follow them. If your stance allows you to fight them, they are attacked instead
 Right-click player A menu: Look, Follow, Attack, Trade and Message. Attack is greyed, with the reason, when you may not fight them
 Click a person The world’s people, whose names are orange, can be talked to. A click walks you up to them and opens a menu of what you can ask: quests first, marked with a ! (ticked once done), then Trade if they keep a shop, then whatever else they will talk about. You can also just say things to them in the chat when you are within three tiles
 Right-click Pick up what lies there, at any distance — you walk over and take it — or look at the tile if there is nothing to pick up. On a bag slot, a stored item or a button it opens that thing’s menu
 Shift+click Look at a tile: who or what is there. Looking at a player names their level and the gear they are using; looking at an item gives its numbers, the same as its tooltip. Pressing both mouse buttons together does the same
 Long-press On a touch screen, holding a finger down is the right-click
 Tab / Next target Target the next monster, nearest first
 Shift+Tab / Next player Target the next player, nearest first: attacked if your stance allows it, followed if not
 Esc / Stop Drop the target and stop attacking, chasing, following and walking
 Drag the world Hold the left button down and move, or drag one finger, to look around. The view stays there until you take a step
 Back to you / Reset view The mouse wheel over the world, or a pinch, zooms. The mark just left of the floor chip in the top right corner of the world puts things back: Back to you once you have dragged the view away or are looking at another floor, Reset view once only the zoom is off, back to the everyday view, which fits 11 tiles across the narrower side of the world wherever the tiles can stay between 32 and 64 pixels
 Trade Choose Trade from the menu on another player within two tiles. Once they accept, both offers show side by side: click a bag slot to offer one, right-click for the pile, click your offer to take one back. When both of you press Accept, everything changes hands at once. Any change takes the accepts back; walking apart or Cancel ends it
 Stance The stance button in Controls says whom you may attack: a white hand , only whoever attacked you; a yellow hand , those and anybody protection zone locked; a red fist , anybody. A new character is white
 Auto What you swing at without picking it: none , only your target; aware , monsters in reach that know you are there; all , any monster in reach. Never another player. Something you swing at by reflex is outlined in yellow
 Chase On, you walk after whatever you pick a fight with. Off, you fight from where you stand — good for holding a doorway or shooting from range
 Ride If you have a horse, the horse’s head below the info button gets you on and off it. It is red, with the reason, while you cannot ride
 F1 / ? Show this help
The screen
Statuses Round badges in the top left corner of the world say what is true of you right now: protected, in a fight, protection zone locked, skulled, hasted, lit, poisoned, burning and so on. Hover or hold one for what it means and how long it has left
 Info panel Your Stats, your Skills and the Players list, behind the info button in the top left corner. On a wide screen it stands down the left of the page; on a phone it opens above the world. Drag its edge to resize it
 Panel icons Every panel has an icon: a bag for the Inventory, a tunic for the Equipment, a star for the Spells, crossed swords for the Controls, and while you are at one, a chest for the Depot and coins for a shop. Press an icon to show its panel and again to hide it. Several can be open at once. Drag an icon to move its panel to the other side or change the order. Long-press or right-click the bag or the star to choose how many columns or rows it shows. Each panel has its own help: Stats Skills Players Inventory Equipment Spells Controls Depot Vendor Market
 Inventory Your bag, in the Inventory panel. Click a slot to use it, right-click (or hold) it for its menu: use, drop, sell while a shop is open, or store at the depot. Ctrl+right-click drops the whole pile
 Within reach Whatever lies under you or next to you shows as an icon in the bottom right corner of the world. Click one to pick it up
 Floor chip The rounded piece in the top right corner says which floor you are seeing: G for the ground, +1 for a floor up, −2 for two down. Press it to look at the floor above or below without moving
 Area names When you walk into a new area, its name shows over the world for a few seconds
 The ⋯ menu In the top right corner: sound and music, pixel font, pixel scaling (capped is quicker on a phone that struggles), button size, debug, highscores, changes, the encyclopedia, your world’s Discord channel, this help and log out. Your choices are remembered in this browser
 Button size In the ⋯ menu: how big the panels’ squares and buttons are, 44, 38 or 32 pixels. Smaller fits more into a panel and leaves more of the screen to the world
 Debug In the ⋯ menu: the numbers behind things. The sum behind every blow you give or take goes to the log under the debug filter, squares round you and every monster show how far each hears and is heard, and the Speed , Clank , Position , HP regen and Mana regen rows are behind it too. Off by default
 Full screen The four corners in the top right corner of the world; Escape gives the screen back. On a phone, press and hold it for its name. On an iPhone, add the game to your home screen instead
 On a phone Long-press stands in for right-click everywhere. The panels open from the row of icons under the world, and the chat line opens from the speech bubble in the log’s corner. The game fits either way you hold the phone
 Sound Blows, misses, arrows, spells and levels all have sounds, quieter the further off they happen. There is quiet music under them too, and it changes with where you are. Both can be turned off in the ⋯ menu
 Colours Every character has a colour of their own: tunic, name and the damage they deal. Monster names and monster damage are red; the names of the people you can talk to are orange
 Ping On the stats panel: how long the server takes to answer you. Green under 100 ms, amber to 300, red past it
 FPS On the stats panel: how many times a second the world is drawn. If it is low, try Pixel scaling on capped
 Highscores In the ⋯ menu: the top characters by level, by each skill, by gold, deaths, kills and more. Click a name for that character’s page
 Changes In the ⋯ menu, and on the login screen: what has changed in the game, newest at the bottom, grouped by day. Anything new since you were last in is marked
 Encyclopedia In the ⋯ menu: tables of the weapons and armor in the game, with what each does. Press a column’s heading to sort by it, and type in the box to find a piece by name
 Worlds There is more than one world, and they share nothing: each has its own characters. The login screen shows every world with its ping and how many are online; pick the one with the lowest ping, or the one your friends play in
 Discord The game has a Discord server for talking with other players and for its news. Join us on Discord at the bottom of the login screen opens your world’s channel
 Log out From the ⋯ menu. You can always log out in a protection zone, and elsewhere once thirty seconds have passed since your last fight. You cannot log out while protection zone locked. The button is greyed while you may not, and its hover says why
Chat
Enter Chat — Enter again to send, Esc to cancel. Everyone within 15 tiles of you on your floor hears you, and your words hang over your head. On a phone, tap the speech bubble in the log’s top right corner
 Yell The loudspeaker button at the start of the chat line, or start a line with /y : said in capitals and heard twice as far
 Private messages /w Name words (or /tell, /msg) sends one player a message wherever they are; /r words answers whoever last wrote to you. You can also choose Message from the menu on a player, a name in the Players list or a line in the log
 Shift+Up / Shift+Down In the chat box, step back through what you have said before
 Filters The button at the end of the chat line chooses which kinds of message the log shows: say, private, noise, damage dealt and received, loot, levels, events, server messages, spells, debug and more. Noise and debug start switched off. The number beside each switch is how many lines of that kind the log holds, hidden ones included. The 𝕋 button beside it puts each line’s kind at its start
 The log’s height The console is directly under the world and as wide as it. Drag the strip between them to make the log taller or shorter
 Newest messages Scroll the log back and it stays put however much comes in; the arrow in its corner, with a count of new lines, takes you back to the newest
 Copying from the log Right-click a line, or hold a finger on it, and choose Copy message . The same menu replies to whoever said it, or looks at them
 /commands Every command you can use, with what it does. A word after it keeps only the ones with that word in them
 /stats Your numbers: level, experience, health, mana, gold, food, deaths, kills, skills and most cast spells
 /kills and /deaths The players you have killed, and what has killed you, newest first, with the level each fell at
 /rates The experience and training rate the world is running at, and how long any temporary rate has left
 /race Choose your race again at your next login
 /password Type /password and your new password to change it. Nobody else sees the line
How things work
Race Every character is an elf, a man, a dwarf or a hobbit, chosen when you first log in. Each people is good at something and pays for it somewhere else. Man : 10 hitpoints and 5 mana a level, and every skill learnt a tenth faster. Elf : 8 hitpoints but 7 mana a level, magic and ranged learnt a fifth faster, quieter than a man, and snow is no slower than grass underfoot. Dwarf : 12 hitpoints but only 4 mana a level, melee and shielding learnt a fifth faster. Hobbit : 8 hitpoints and 5 mana a level, ranged and fishing learnt a fifth faster, the quietest, and a meal lasts half as long again. Every race is medium, an orc’s size. /race lets you choose again. A character with no race counts as a man until they choose
 Hitpoints What you can take before you fall: 100 at level 1, and more every level (see Race). You heal over time while you are fed. The bar over a creature is drawn bigger the tougher it is, so you can see what you are walking up to. While a potion is working, the pale stretch on your bar is the healing still to come
 Mana What spells cost. A level one has none; every level adds some (see Race). It comes back slowly while you are fed, faster as you level, but not in a protection zone. A mana potion puts some back over a few seconds
 Food You heal and get mana back only while fed. You do not eat by hand: whenever your stomach is empty, the bag eats a food for you. With nothing to eat you lose a hitpoint every 10 seconds, down to your last, except in a protection zone
 Potions Click one in your bag to drink it. A health potion heals and a mana potion restores mana, over a few seconds rather than at once
 Experience and levels Killing monsters earns experience, which floats up over your head. Everybody who hurt a monster gets its whole experience. Each level gives you more hitpoints, mana, speed and carrying capacity, and makes health and mana come back faster. The stats panel shows your XP rate an hour and how long the next level will take
 Skills Fist, melee, ranged, shielding, magic and fishing. Each is trained by using it: every swing, shot, blocked blow or catch. Magic is trained by spending mana. Higher skill makes your gear hit, block or catch better. See the Skills panel’s own help
 Spells A spell is cast by saying its words into the chat box, or by pressing its icon in the Spells panel, which lists every spell your mana can pay for, cheapest first. Hover or hold an icon for what it does. Green will go off, gold will go off but waste the mana, red will be refused, with the reason. Spells have cooldowns. Everyone near you hears the words. A spell that calls up a creature opens a list of the ones you can call; press the ☆ beside one to pin it to the top of that list, and the ★ to unpin it
 Fighting Your attack, defense and armor are on the stats panel. Every blow is rolled: it can do anything from a little to its most. Your defense — your shield’s block and your weapon’s parry — meets each blow first, but only the first two blows every two seconds, so a crowd gets past it. Your armor meets every blow after that. Shots go past your defense and meet your armor alone, and do not count among those two blows. A shield is armor as well as a block: its block, before skill, counts in your armor too. Changing what is in your hands takes a moment : a weapon just taken up swings no sooner than a second later, and a shield just raised blocks nothing for a second. The log says what hit you and with what
 Ranged weapons A bow or crossbow shoots your target from a distance, along a clear line: over water, but not through walls. Every shot uses an arrow or bolt from your bag, and with none you do not shoot. Shots miss more often while you are moving; stand still to shoot true. A shot goes past a monster’s defense and meets its armor alone, as shots at you meet only yours
 Cleave and reach A weapon marked cleave carries a killing blow on: whatever the blow had left over after the kill goes into the nearest monster within the weapon’s reach, where its armor and defense meet it as they would any blow, and if that kills it too the rest goes on again. It never goes into a person. A long weapon marked reach strikes that many tiles away along a clear line, thrust rather than shot
 Poison and other harm Some attacks poison, burn, slow or hold you. Poison bites every two seconds, a little less each time, and armor is no answer to it. The badge in the top left shows it, and your health bar turns green for what the poison will still take. Dying clears it
 Noise and sight Monsters notice you by what they hear and see. Your clank , on the stats panel, is how much noise you make; the less, the closer you can get before something notices you. A creature can only see you within its sight, and at night or underground that is short unless you carry a light
 Running away A monster follows you only so far before it gives up and wanders home, but while you keep hitting it, it keeps after you. Some monsters run when badly hurt
 The dark Under the ground, and above it at night, you see only what is lit and the tile or so round you. A torch held in your off hand lights the ground round you, and so does a light spell. A torch burns only while held in the dark, and the next one in your bag is lit when it gutters out. A light can be seen from as far as it reaches
 Day and night Above the ground, ten minutes of day, then three of night, the same for everybody
 Protection zones Safe ground, such as the starting village, towns and the ground round the world’s people. Nothing can be fought there, monsters cannot come in, and you never starve, but mana does not come back. The shield badge shows while you stand in one
 Where you start New characters wake in Hobbiton’s protection zone with a little gear, food and a potion, and that is where you come back after dying
 Logging back in You come back where you logged out, or on the nearest free tile
 Death Dying costs a tenth of your experience and of the practice under every skill, and every coin you carry spills where you fell. Now and then something else in your bag drops too. Your gear and everything else come back with you to the temple. Store what you value in the depot
 Fighting players Your stance says whom you may attack. Whoever you attack may fight back. Nobody may attack a player under half their level unless that player wears a skull. Attacking a player who is not protection zone locked and has not attacked you locks you: your eyes go red, and you cannot log out until the fight is over. Killing such a player puts a skull on you for a time, and a skull keeps you out of protection zones
 Bosses A boss is a monster played by an admin: its name is in the monsters’ colour and a look says it is a boss. Fight it as you would a monster
 Gear The best weapon, shield, armor, helm, cloak and belt in your bag are used for you, and outlined. Click a piece to use that one instead; click it again to go back to the best. Rings are worn only when you click them. Hover any item for what it does. The colour of the outline round an item says who made it, and its tooltip says what that adds
 Wear and tear Gear can break in use. When it does it is gone, and the next best thing in your bag takes over
 Weight Everything weighs something, counted in coins (cn), the weight of one gold coin. What you can carry grows with your level, and a belt adds more. The bag’s icon fills as you load it. Over the line you keep everything but can pick nothing more up. Weight never slows you
 Your bag Holds any number of kinds of thing, limited only by weight. Everything stacks
 Loot A monster sometimes leaves loot beside its body when it dies: gold and other things, picked up off the ground like anything else. A body lasts a few minutes, then rots away
 Shops Some of the world’s people keep shops: click them and choose Trade. They sell their wares and buy most things for a fifth of what they are worth. While a shop is open your bag shows what it pays for each pile
 Depot Storage boxes stand in several towns, and every box opens the same depot. Stand beside one and press the chest icon. It holds anything, as much as you like, and what is in it is safe when you die
 Market The market is open at every depot box, for trading with other players: list things from your depot at a price, or buy what others have listed. Unsold prices fall over time. Gold from sales waits to be collected
 Quests Many of the world’s people want something: ask them about what they talk about. A quest pays once per life, and dying lets you do it again. When somebody sends you somewhere, it is marked on your map
 Your map The map fills in as you explore and is kept with your character. Zoom out for the map of where you have been. Click into the black to walk there if there is a way
 Floors Stairs, ramps, ladders and holes take you up and down. Buildings show their roofs from outside, and lift them while you are inside. Use the floor chip to look up or down
 Somebody in your way Walk into another player standing still and they step aside. A monster smaller than you is pushed aside too; one your own size or bigger stands its ground. Walking into things pushes them and never crushes them
 Fishing Take a fishing rod in hand and it casts by itself at any fish you can see within a few tiles. Every catch trains fishing
 Dropped skulls A death leaves a skull where it happened, its eyes in the colour of whoever died
 Announcements Everyone online is told when a player enters or leaves the world, is slain, or advances a level
Choose your race
Every character is one of the free peoples of Middle-earth, and each is good at something and pays for it somewhere else. Your choice is kept; while races are not locked in, /race brings this window back at your next login. The help’s Race entry has the numbers.
Elf The Firstborn. More mana, quicker at magic and the bow, and snow is no hindrance; less health.
 Man The Followers. Ordinary health and mana, and a tenth quicker to learn every skill.
 Dwarf Durin’s folk. The most health, quicker at melee and shielding; little mana.
 Hobbit Halflings of the Shire. Hardly heard, a meal goes half as far again, quicker with the bow and the rod; less health.

Your stats
The bars
Name and level The title is who you are and how far you have come. Every level is ten more hitpoints and five more mana for a man; the Race entry in the help has the other peoples’ numbers. It does not change what you can carry: the bag is twenty-five slots for everybody
 Health What you can take before you fall. While a potion is working on you, the stretch of bar past where you are now is the healing still to come: it is where the window will leave you when it runs out
 Mana What is left to cast spells with. A character starts with none and holds more every level, and gets it back slowly, faster every level, but only while you are fed and not inside a protection zone . Spending it trains the magic skill. A mana potion puts some back over a few seconds
 Experience How far through the level you are, and how much is left to the next
 Food The bar is what is left of the meal you are on. It is spent only while wounds are closing, so at full health it does not run down, and when it runs out the food that will free a square of your bag soonest is eaten by itself. The Food row under it is the whole of it: that meal and every meal in your bag, added up — how long you can go on mending before you have to find more.
The rows
Hold a row Every row here, and the meal bar, has a hover saying what it means and how yours comes to it. Hold the row on a phone, or right-click it, and that hover is written in the message log, under the row’s name, to read at leisure or copy
 XP rate The experience you are earning an hour , worked out from what you made in the last fifteen minutes , so it shows how well the way you are hunting pays now rather than all evening: move to a better spot and it climbs within minutes, walk back to town and it runs down. It is counted on the clock, so time spent standing about, resting or walking counts against it as it does in a hunt. It starts over when you log in, reload the page or die, and reads – until it has watched a minute; until fifteen minutes have gone by it is the rate over however long it has watched
 Next level How long the next level will take at the XP rate above: 40 min , 2 h 05 min . It reads – while you are earning nothing
 Attack What one swing of yours takes off, before their armor, written as the range a swing rolls: 0–24 is anywhere from 0 to 24, evenly. Your swings roll from 0 , so one can come to nothing; a monster’s roll starts at a fifth of its top, rounded up. Now and then a swing is a critical : it rolls from the top to twice the top instead, and CRIT! goes up over whoever it lands on, in the colour of whoever landed it, the same as their damage numbers: yours for your swing, red for a monster’s. Monsters crit on the same terms. Spells never crit. It is the weapon in hand scaled by your skill with it, or your bare hands scaled by fisting when there is nothing in them — a weapon replaces your fists rather than adding to them. A fist is worth 5, four fifths of it untrained, and all of it once you have punched your way to fisting 2, so bare hands read 0–4 at first. A weapon that hits some creatures harder adds the range it rolls against them in brackets on the line under it. Its hover shows your own sum , ending on the number the row shows
 Defense The range your shield’s block and weapon’s parry are rolled in against each blow, written like Attack’s: 0–8 means every blow loses a whole number anywhere from 0 to 8, evenly, before your armor meets the rest, so it can come up empty. Only the first two blows in any two seconds are met by it; the rest meet your armor alone. Shots are never met by it, and do not count among the two. The top is your weapon’s parry × (8 + melee)/10 plus your shield × (8 + shielding)/10. With nothing in your off hand, your bare hands dodge 2 × (8 + fisting)/10 instead of a shield, beside your weapon’s parry. A two-handed weapon that parries takes the off hand and the dodge with it; a bow, which parries nothing, still dodges
 Armor What a blow loses to what you wear before anything else is asked: a roll between half this, rounded up, and this , which is natural armor plus the armor on your back, the cloak over it, the helm on your head and your shield’s block, before skill. The number is the ceiling, as a weapon’s attack is — a suit always soaks at least half what is written on it, and sometimes all of it. A blow no bigger than the floor shows sparks ; one the roll only stops by going above it is a puff. It is rolled against every blow that connects, and a suit is on your back whichever way a blow comes, so it has a floor where your defense does not. The row is written as that roll, 7–14 for armor 14, the way Attack and Defense are; bare skin is a plain 0. It is on the panel at every width, debug or not
 HP regen How fast your health comes back on its own, in points a minute: faster every level, only while you are fed, but in a protection zone fed or not. 0 (hungry) is an empty stomach and nothing in the bag to eat. Potions are not counted. The row is only there with debug on
 Mana regen The same for mana: faster every level, only while you are fed, and never in a protection zone, where it reads 0 (protection zone) ; a level one, who holds no mana, reads 0 (no mana yet) . Potions are not counted. The row is only there with debug on
 Speed How fast you walk, in tiles a second. It grows a little every level. A diagonal step takes longer than a straight one, and the ground counts too, for you and monsters alike: a road or a bridge is quicker than grass, sand a little slower, snow and swamp much slower. The row is only there with debug on
 Clank The noise your gear makes, in tiles: every monster notices you this much further off than it would somebody wearing nothing at all. The pieces in use, added up, though quiet ones take off only what the quietest of them does; what is only in the bag is silent. It is always shown on the stats panel. Its hover shows your own sum : your racial clank, each loud piece and the quietest quiet one, and the tile a great ring takes off.
 Sight How far you make others out, in tiles, written dark / light . You see something only if you hear it, and then within your dark sight if it stands in the dark, or out to your light sight if it stands in a light. Above the ground by day the sky lights everything; at night it is as under the ground. Other players you see as far as you can hear them, lit or not. Monsters notice you the same way: they have to hear you and see you
 Stance Whom you may attack: white , only whoever attacked you; yellow , those and anybody protection zone locked; red , anybody. Changed with the stance button in Controls; see Fighting players
 Ping The last round trip to the world: how long it took the server to hear a ping and answer it, through the same loop that moves you. Under 100 ms nothing notices; past 300 your steps and swings arrive late
 FPS Frames a second: how many times a second the world is drawn, counted over the last second. A phone draws at most 60; under 30 the world starts to stutter
 Position Where you stand, as three numbers: the tile across, counted from the west edge of the map, the tile down, counted from the north edge, and the floor, 0 being the ground, +1 the floor above and −1 the one under it. The row is only there with debug on
 Statuses Everything currently true of you, written out in full: in a fight, in a protection zone, what spell is still up and how long is left of it. They stack onto as many lines as they need — a status nobody can read is not a shorter status, it is a missing one — and they never make the panel wider, so the panel beside them keeps its place on a phone
Every bar and row says its own piece on its tooltip, so hover one for just that.

Inventory
Opening it
Where the slots are In the Inventory panel , which the bag in the row of icons under the world shows and hides.
 What is behind it The world, still being played. The box has no wash under it, it takes no keys, and the arrows still walk you while it is open — so a potion can be drunk out of it while you are backing away from something
 In a trade It opens by itself when a trade window or a shop does, since you trade out of it, stays shown for as long as the window is up, and closes again at the end unless you had it open already
Clicks
Click a slot Use what is in it: drink a potion, wield a weapon, take up a shield, put on armor, a cloak or a helmet, cast a fishing rod at the water beside you
 Shift+click a slot Look at what is in it, the same way shift+clicking the ground looks at what is lying there. The answer is the same sentence either way, since it is the same thing however you came to be holding it — but it goes to the console alone, without floating over your head, since your eyes are on the bag. It asks rather than does: a shift+click never wields, drinks, eats or offers. Pressing both mouse buttons together on a slot does the same
 Right-click a slot A little menu on the item saying what it is and offering every way of using or letting go of it — drop or sell as many as you pick, or store, Equip it ( Unequip once it is on, which leaves its place empty as Take it off on the Equipment panel does), or Pin it to the top of the bag ( Unpin once it is pinned). Sorting the whole bag by weight, or back by kind, is on the bag icon instead: right-click or hold it. A dropped thing lands at your feet, or on the nearest tile with room on it, since it looks for clean ground rather than landing in the blood
 Ctrl+right-click Drop the whole pile at once. A slot is a pile, and a click that put all forty rocks on the floor is not a click to make by accident
 Long press The same as a right-click: the same menu, on a touchscreen
 At the depot Standing on the depot floor, every click on a bag slot stores it instead of using or dropping it, and a click in the Depot panel takes it back out — the whole of it a click, if you can lift it. Holding a slot either way asks how many
What is in it
Any number of kinds The bag holds any number of kinds of thing, the same for everybody; its one limit is the weight. The depot has no limit either, since it is a building rather than a back
 Stacks Everything stacks, so a slot is a pile of one kind: five hundred rocks are one slot, and five thousand gold coins are one slot.
 How full it is The bag’s icon says how full it is, by weight: see the next entry. Hover over the panel between the squares and it says how many kinds of thing you have, such as “12 kinds of thing”. Only the slots with something in them are drawn, so the grid shows what you carry
 The fill on the bag’s icon The bag’s icon fills from its foot as far up as your bag is full: what it weighs, of what you can lift. The fill is muted while there is room, turns amber from four fifths of the way, and past the line it is red and hatched , full to the top, with a red border. Hover the icon for the numbers — what you carry against what you can lift in cn , the percent, and how much is free or how far over you are — and what the weight does; long-press or right-click it for the same, with a button to show or hide the panel. Weight never slows you: all it does is leave what would take you past the line where it is. Over the line you keep everything and walk as fast as ever, but nothing more goes into your bag until you drop, sell or store enough to be back under it; a sale or a trade that leaves you lighter still goes through.
 Outlined slots What you are actually fighting with: the best weapon, shield, armor and helm in the bag are used without being equipped, and the best cloak over the armor with them. A click picks one as your choice and a click on a picked one puts it away and leaves you bare, which stays that way until you say otherwise. Something better picked up for the first time goes on by itself , over a choice too, and what it replaces stays in the bag
 Pinned kinds Right-click or hold a slot and choose Pin , and that kind of thing goes to the top of the bag, with a small gold dot in the slot’s top right corner; Unpin in the same place puts it back among the rest. The pinned kinds come first and everything else after them, each lot in the bag’s own order, by kind or by weight. A pin is on the kind, not the pile, so it stays when you run out and the next one you pick up goes to the top again. Your pins are kept with your character, so they follow you to another device
 Tooltips Hovering a slot says what the piece does, what it weighs, how likely it is to break, and which click it is about to be. A shift+click on it says the same numbers after its sentence
 Weight Everything you can carry weighs something, counted in coins (cn) — the weight of one gold coin. The bag’s icon says what your bag comes to against what you can lift, and the tooltip on each slot says the piece’s own weight and the pile’s. What you can lift grows with your level: 10 000 cn at level one and 500 more for every level after it, and a belt at your waist adds to it. Gold is what fills a bag, more than gear: hauling a hoard home is a trip to plan, and the depot , which is never weighed, is where the rest of it waits. A pile on the ground too heavy to take whole is taken as far as you can carry and the rest left lying. Nothing is ever taken out of your bag to keep you under the line: over the limit, you keep everything you have and simply cannot pick up more until you put something down

Your skills
How they grow
By use Every swing, shot, punch, blow taken behind a shield or cast of the line is a go at the skill it belongs to. Hover a row for what the level on it does, how far you are to the next and, once it has moved lately, about how long the next will take at your pace over the last two minutes . Hold a row on a phone, or right-click it, and the hover is written in the log. Time away costs nothing; dying costs some of the practice under every skill
 Magic Counted in mana rather than in goes: one use for every point spent, whatever it was spent on. Higher magic makes damage spells hit harder. Some gear counts as levels more while it is in use: what it adds is written in blue after the level, so 12 +5 is twelve earned and five lent. Hover the row, or hold it on a touchscreen, for what adds what
What they do
Melee and ranged Skill multiplies your gear: attack × (8 + skill)/10, for melee and ranged alike, so four fifths of the attack untrained, all of it at 2 and twice it at 12. Melee scales a weapon’s parry the same way
 Shielding A shield puts its block × (8 + shielding)/10 into your defense, the top of the roll every blow by hand meets before your armor does, which is the Defense row on the stats panel, and its block as it is into your armor. Shots go past the defense, so only blows by hand teach shielding, when the shield takes something off them
 Fisting The same arithmetic on the 5 an empty hand hits for: 3 untrained, 5 at fisting 4, 11 at 20. It scales the dodge of 2 your bare hands have when your off hand is empty the same way, and a blow the dodge takes something off trains fisting
 Fishing Take a fishing rod in hand and it casts by itself at any fish in reach, on your own level or in plain view below you from a roof or a hilltop: every cast is a go at it

Players
Who Everybody in the world right now, you included, with their levels: the tallest first, and by name where two are level, the same list and order the Online row opens. Your own row is picked out. It keeps itself up to date — somebody arriving or leaving, a level gained or lost — without anything to click
 What they play on For admins, and for your own row: under each name, the browser and system they are playing on — Firefox on Windows, Safari on iPhone — as their browser describes itself when they connect. Nothing is shown for somebody whose browser said nothing recognisable
 Hidden A shut eye on your own row, just before the platform marks, means you are off everybody else’s list: an admin hid you with /hide, which takes you off the highscores too, or, for an admin, you went /invisible. You still see yourself here, since this is where you check you are in the world at all, but nobody else does. Hover it, or press and hold it on a touch screen, for which it is and how to come back: an admin’s /show for /hide, /visible for /invisible. Only your own row ever carries it
 Skulls A skull beside a name is the same mark that is beside it in the world, with how long it has left: they killed somebody who was not protection zone locked. They stay locked while it runs, so no protection zone will take them, and attacking them locks you out of nothing. Nothing beside a name is no skull
 Experience A player who dies loses a tenth of their experience. If whoever killed them was a lower level, that tenth goes to the killers: shared by the damage each did, among everybody lower than the one who fell. Killing somebody at your level or below pays nothing
 Clicking a name Everybody else’s name is underlined, since it is something to press. A click opens the same menu as a right-click on them in the world — look, follow, attack, trade — while they are in sight of you. Somebody further off is too far away for any of those, so a click on them opens the spells said at them by name
