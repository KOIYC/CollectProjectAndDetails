Planning
 A route for the bike you are on
 Tap waypoints on the map and the route is calculated on the phone, on downloaded 5°×5° tiles. Touring, road, gravel, MTB and direct profiles, up to three alternatives, the elevation profile and a breakdown of the surfaces you will ride on. The route can be coloured by gradient, surface, wind or rain, and one time slider sets when you leave and moves the weather on the map and along the route.

 Elevation profile and surface statistics for every option
 An online routing server is optional, for areas with no tiles
Loops
 “A 60 km round trip from here via the lake”
 Say how far you want to ride and, if you like, one place to pass. Candidates are generated on the phone and scored on length, climbing, surface and repeated roads, so what comes back is a ride, not a detour.
 Round trips that come back to where you started

Offline
 The map, the routing and the search, with no connection
 Each routing tile brings a search index of its places, streets with house numbers and points of interest: cafés, drinking water, toilets, repair stations, shelters, bike shops, campsites, hotels, passes, peaks, viewpoints, stations and landmarks. Typing “water” lists the nearest taps by distance, and typos are corrected from the index itself.

Navigation
 Spoken turns, on the phone
 A turn banner and spoken cues while you ride a route, and when you leave it, your choice of a way back onto the plan, a new route to the destination or just the direction back, all worked out on the phone. Compass or north-up follow modes. The cues are generated from the same routing data that planned the ride, so they work where there is no network. A GPX route brings its own cue sheet and points of interest along, and the record sheet adds an elevation profile with the climb ahead, the arrival time and the cue list as swipeable pages. A GPX without turns gets them created on the phone from the map.

Recording
 It keeps recording when the phone does not
 Recording runs in the background with a foreground notification on Android and a live activity on iOS, and survives the app being killed. Battery saver dims the screen and coarsens the GPS. Heart rate, cadence and power come along from an Apple Watch, a Bluetooth sensor or your health app, if you switch them on. Afterwards, ride pages with elevation, speed, heart rate, splits sized to the ride, the climbs you rode, the surface under your wheels from the offline map, and a speed-coloured track; on request calories, heart-rate and power zones, normalised power and an estimated power for rides without a meter.

Sensors
 Sensors and your watch
 Heart rate, cadence and power come from a Bluetooth sensor — a strap, a speed and cadence sensor or a power meter — paired once and then part of every ride: on the record screen while you ride, and kept with the ride afterwards. The Velorki app on your Apple Watch shows the ride on your wrist and sends your heart rate to the phone; start, pause and finish from the watch, and it keeps measuring through your stops, or rests at every stop if you prefer. Apple Health and Health Connect can supply a heart rate too, and all of it is free, all of it optional, and nothing happens until you switch a source on.

Velorki Plus
 Ask for the ride you want
 Plus is the optional subscription for what needs our servers. Most of it is the assistant: you say what you have in mind, and the route is still planned on your phone.
 Describe a ride
 “A gravel loop of about 80 km on quiet roads” becomes a request the planner understands: a distance, a shape, places to pass. The route itself is planned on your phone.
Ask about the route on the map
 Have the route checked, find coffee around halfway or a tap to refill. Each finding has its place on the map, and Add as stop or Avoid changes the route in one step that Undo takes back.
A description for your routes
 Describe this route writes a paragraph from what the phone knows about the route: its climbs, surfaces, towns and the cafés along it.
Share links
 Share a route or a ride as a link that opens in any browser, with the map, the stats and a GPX download.
Finer weather along the route
 The free weather along the route comes from a coarser public forecast. Plus asks local weather services instead — the US National Weather Service, the Deutscher Wetterdienst's station forecasts in Germany, MET Norway elsewhere — for finer wind, rain and temperature, with gusts and the chance of rain.
Rain radar across Europe
 Where the free map has only satellite estimates, such as in France, Spain, Italy, the UK, Switzerland and Scandinavia, Plus shows real radar from EUMETNET OPERA and Radar-DPC (Italy): 1 km cells, a new image every 5 minutes, the last 2 hours.
The assistant asks before it sends anything: what you type and, only if you allow it, your position rounded to about a kilometre. No account, no route history.
 Monthly or yearly through the App Store or Google Play, with a free trial. The Plus page lists the price in your country.

Questions
 Frequently asked
 Do I need an account?
 + No. There is no account and no sign-up, not even for Velorki Plus — a subscription is restored through the store you bought it in, without an account with us. Sync, if you want it, goes through your own iCloud or Google account.
 Does it really work without a connection?
 + Yes. Download a region once and the map, the routing, the place search and the spoken turns all run on the phone. Only the weather, the assistant, sync, the Strava and Ride with GPS connections and share links need a network.
 What does Velorki cost?
 + Everything that runs on the phone is free: planning, loops, offline maps and routing, search, navigation, recording, heart rate, cadence and power from your sensors, ride stats, the weather map, the weather along the route from a public forecast, GPX, FIT and TCX files, sync through your own iCloud or Google Drive, and the Strava and Ride with GPS connections. Velorki Plus is a subscription for the server-side features; the Plus page lists its monthly and yearly price per country, as the App Store and Google Play show them.
 What is Velorki Plus?
 + A small subscription for the parts that need our servers: the assistant, share links, finer weather along the route from local weather services and rain radar across Europe. It is bought, managed and cancelled in the App Store or Google Play.
 What leaves my phone?
 + Nothing, unless you ask for it. Routes, rides and settings are stored on the device; when you sync them, they go to your own iCloud or Google Drive, never to us. The assistant asks for consent before any text leaves, and rounds the position it sends to about a kilometre. Map tiles and online search are fetched only when you are not using downloaded data.
 Where does the map data come from?
 + From OpenStreetMap, under the ODbL. The map, the routing and the place search all run on that open data; the projects and licences behind it are on the credits page.
 Credits and licences
 Is Velorki really open source?
 + Yes, so anyone can check what it does with your data. The app, its pure-Dart packages, the relay and this website are AGPL-3.0-only; all of it is on GitHub. The name, logo and icon are reserved, so a fork has to rebrand before publishing it to a store.
 Can I run the server parts myself?
 + Yes. The relay, a routing server and a tile host can all be self-hosted; the repository has the compose files and the systemd units. A fork that points at its own relay needs no subscription at all.
