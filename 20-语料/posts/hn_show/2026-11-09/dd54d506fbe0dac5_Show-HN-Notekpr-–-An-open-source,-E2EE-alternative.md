## Private by design
Even we can't read your notes.
Everything is encrypted on your devices before it leaves them: note text, titles, file names, attachments, vault names, even who is looking at which note. The server, ours or yours, orders and stores ciphertext. Removing someone renews the keys, and every change is signed by the person who made it.

🔑 Keys stay on your devices
 Your account key never leaves them. A new device is approved from one you already use, with a code to compare, or unlocked with your recovery key.
✍️ Signed, not just encrypted
 The vault's owner signs who belongs to it, and every edit is signed by its author: a server can't add people or forge changes.
🧭 Honest about the rest
 The server still learns sizes, timing and who shares with whom. The security page lists all of it, so you can judge for yourself.

## Questions
Fair questions.
Can you read my notes? No. Note text, titles, file and folder names, attachments and vault names are encrypted on your devices with keys the server never gets, on our servers and on yours alike. The server does learn some metadata, such as sizes, timing and who shares with whom; the security page lists exactly what.
Is anything kept for paying plans? No. Every plan, Free included, has every feature: the editor, drawings, sharing and editing together, comments, history, offline use, the command line and AI agents. Paying gets you more room, or a server of your own. Hosting it yourself, the only limits are the ones you set.
Can I try it first? Yes: the demo has people, shared vaults and notes in it already. Sign in as alice or bob, or any name, then open a second tab as someone else and edit together. Anyone can sign in as anyone there, and it starts over every hour, so keep nothing of yours in it.
What if I lose my phone and my laptop? Your recovery key, shown once when you set up, unlocks your account on a new device. Otherwise, new devices are approved from one you already use.
Can I take my notes and leave? Any time. Export a vault as Markdown, keep a synced folder of files on your disk, or export everything as one .zip and import it on another server: ours to yours, or back. Even without notekpr, an export and your recovery key are all you need to get your notes out as Markdown files.
Is it really open source? Yes, all of it, the server, the web app and the command-line client, under the AGPL. Read it, run it, check it: the docs explain how it's built and why.
Does it work with Obsidian? Yes. The command-line client keeps your vaults as a folder of Markdown that Obsidian opens as a vault, synced both ways. Importing an Obsidian vault brings its attachments and turns its Excalidraw drawings into drawings.
Does it work offline? Yes. The browser keeps an encrypted copy of your vaults, and what you write offline syncs and merges when you are back.
Is it ready? It's young, and it works: it is tested end to end, and known issues are tracked in the open. Hosted plans open soon; today you can try the demo or host it yourself. Keep backups of what matters, as with any new tool.
