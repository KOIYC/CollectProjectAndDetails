# How five years of war changed the world's oil and gas

I wanted to see where the world's energy actually goes, and how much that changed after Russia invaded Ukraine. So I put about 80 oil, fuel and gas routes on a globe and animated them from mid-2021 to today. Press play and the months tick by. When something big happens, the camera flies over, you see a short reconstruction, and the pipes change right in front of you.

## How to read the map

Crude oil measured in mb/d Refined products measured in mb/d LNG (seaborne gas) measured in Mt/yr Pipeline gas measured in bcm/yr

Thicker pipe, more energy. I convert everything to barrels of oil a day, so a gas pipeline and a tanker lane can be compared side by side.

Brighter pipe and faster dots, closer to full. "Full" means the most that route has ever carried. A route running at 10% of that looks faint and its dots crawl. Nothing glows or pulses just for show. If a pipe gets dimmer, it's because less is moving through it.

Red tint means the route goes through a strait that's under attack but still open. Grey means nothing is getting through: the strait is closed, the port is blockaded, or the flow stopped. Dashed grey is a route that's shut or was never used, and the sparse dark dashes are Nord Stream after it was blown up. A ring pulsing around a port or refinery means it was hit or blockaded.

After each event, a small panel lists what changed: which routes went up, down or stopped, with the numbers before and after. Matching numbered markers sit on the pipes themselves, so you can tell them apart even where several routes run close together.

Countries are coloured by what they do: amber for big oil producers, cyan for gas and LNG exporters, pink for refining hubs and violet for big importers. Red stripes mean war or heavy sanctions. The little markers are oil production, gas production, refining hub, LNG export terminal, LNG import terminal, oil terminal / port, pipeline station / hub, maritime chokepoint.

The numbers in the top-left corner just add up the routes on the map. "Oil via Hormuz", for example, counts about 17 of the roughly 20 million barrels a day that really went through before 2026. They're good for seeing direction and size, but they aren't official statistics.

## The timeline

1. 1 Jun 2021

### The pre-war energy map

Before the war, Russia supplied about 40% of the EU’s gas and a quarter of its crude, mostly by pipeline. The Gulf pushed about 20 mb/d of oil through the Strait of Hormuz to Asia, and Qatar, Australia and the US led LNG exports. Press ▶ to watch the map rearrange itself.

2. 1 Oct 2021

### Europe's gas crunch begins

Low storage, a post-COVID rebound and minimal extra Russian flows send Dutch TTF gas prices up five-fold. On 1 November Algeria lets the Maghreb–Europe pipeline contract lapse amid its rift with Morocco. Flows on Yamal–Europe fall and even reverse in December.

Show on the globe ↑
3. 24 Feb 2022

### Russia invades Ukraine

Brent passes $100 for the first time since 2014. Two days earlier Germany froze certification of Nord Stream 2. Gas keeps moving through Ukraine itself under the 2019 transit contract, even as fighting rages around the pipelines.

Show on the globe ↑
4. 8 Mar 2022

### US & UK ban Russian oil; record stock release

Brent spikes to about $128 on 8 March. IEA members release 182 million barrels from emergency stocks, the largest release ever at the time. European buyers start “self-sanctioning” Russian barrels; India begins buying discounted Urals.

Show on the globe ↑
5. 27 Apr 2022

### Gazprom cuts off Poland & Bulgaria

After they refuse to pay in roubles, Gazprom halts supply to Poland and Bulgaria. In May Russia sanctions EuRoPol GAZ, owner of the Polish section of Yamal–Europe, and the pipeline goes dark.

Show on the globe ↑
6. 14 Jun 2022

### Nord Stream throttled to 40%, then 20%

Citing a turbine stuck in Canada, Gazprom cuts Nord Stream 1 to 40% of capacity, then 20% in late July, and halts it entirely on 31 August. A fire at Freeport LNG (8 June) removes about 2% of global LNG supply. TTF peaks above €300/MWh intraday on 26 August.

Show on the globe ↑
7. 26 Sep 2022

### Nord Stream sabotaged

Underwater explosions rupture three of the four Nord Stream strings near Bornholm. No gas was flowing, but the blasts end any prospect of a return to pre-war pipeline trade with Germany.

Show on the globe ↑
8. 1 Oct 2022

### Baltic Pipe opens; Europe pivots to LNG

Baltic Pipe brings Norwegian gas to Poland. Norway becomes Europe’s largest gas supplier, and Germany builds its first LNG terminal (Wilhelmshaven) in under 200 days. US LNG cargoes to Europe triple versus 2021.

Show on the globe ↑
9. 5 Dec 2022

### EU crude embargo and G7 $60 price cap

The EU bans seaborne Russian crude; the G7 caps the price Western shippers and insurers can serve at $60/bbl. Russian Baltic and Black Sea barrels swing to India, China and Turkey through Suez, carried more and more by an aging “shadow fleet”.

Show on the globe ↑
10. 5 Feb 2023

### EU bans Russian diesel and fuels

With Russian diesel gone, Europe buys from the Middle East, India and the US Gulf. Russian diesel heads instead to Turkey, Brazil and Africa. Fuel ends up travelling much further to reach the same customers.

Show on the globe ↑
11. 25 Mar 2023

### Iraq–Turkey pipeline shut

An ICC arbitration ruling in Iraq’s favour leads Turkey to halt the Kirkuk–Ceyhan pipeline, stranding about 450 kb/d of northern Iraqi and Kurdish crude for two and a half years.

Show on the globe ↑
12. 1 Aug 2023

### Drought throttles the Panama Canal

Record-low water in Gatun Lake forces draft and transit limits. US LNG cargoes bound for Asia divert around the Cape of Good Hope, adding weeks to voyages.

Show on the globe ↑
13. 19 Nov 2023

### Houthis attack Red Sea shipping

The Galaxy Leader is seized; by mid-December most Western tanker operators avoid Bab el-Mandeb. Gulf crude and diesel to Europe re-route around Africa, Suez oil transits roughly halve, and QatarEnergy pauses Red Sea LNG transits in January 2024. Russia-linked tankers keep sailing through.

Show on the globe ↑
14. 13 Mar 2024

### Drones hit Russian refineries

Ukraine’s long-range drone campaign hits Kirishi, Ryazan, Volgograd, Tuapse and others; at peak an estimated 10–15% of Russian refining capacity is offline. Moscow bans gasoline exports. Strikes widen in 2025 to Primorsk, Novorossiysk, the CPC terminal and Druzhba pumping stations.

Show on the globe ↑
15. 1 May 2024

### Trans Mountain expansion opens

Canada’s 890 kb/d Trans Mountain line starts up, giving Alberta crude a direct route to the Pacific and Chinese buyers for the first time.

Show on the globe ↑
16. 1 Sep 2024

### Dangote refinery ramps up

Africa’s largest refinery begins producing gasoline in Lagos, eating into Europe’s long-standing gasoline exports to West Africa.

Show on the globe ↑
17. 1 Jan 2025

### Ukraine gas transit ends

Kyiv declines to renew the five-year Gazprom–Naftogaz transit deal. about 15 bcm/yr of flows to Slovakia and Austria stop at midnight; TurkStream becomes the only pipeline carrying Russian gas into the EU.

Show on the globe ↑
18. 13 Jun 2025

### The Israel–Iran “12-day war”

Israel strikes Iran’s nuclear and military sites; the US bombs Fordow on 22 June. Iran’s parliament votes to close Hormuz, but oil keeps moving. Brent jumps to about $79, then falls back after the 24 June ceasefire. Markets shrugged it off.

Show on the globe ↑
19. 27 Sep 2025

### Kirkuk–Ceyhan restarts

After two and a half years, Kurdish crude flows to Ceyhan again under a Baghdad–Erbil–Ankara deal.

Show on the globe ↑
20. 22 Oct 2025

### US sanctions Rosneft & Lukoil

Washington blacklists Russia’s two largest oil companies. Indian refiners cut purchases of Russian crude and return to Gulf suppliers; discounts on Urals widen sharply.

Show on the globe ↑
21. 10 Dec 2025

### US moves on Venezuela’s tanker fleet

US forces seize a sanctioned tanker off Venezuela and Washington declares a blockade of sanctioned tankers serving the country. China-bound exports, which ran through intermediaries and ship-to-ship transfers, collapse within weeks.

Show on the globe ↑
22. 3 Jan 2026

### US captures Maduro; takes control of Venezuelan oil

In an overnight operation US special forces seize Nicolás Maduro in Caracas and fly him to New York for trial. The US says it will run Venezuela’s oil sales and rebuild the industry; licences broaden in March. Exports pivot to the US Gulf Coast and new buyers, reaching about 1.2 mb/d by spring.

Show on the globe ↑
23. 21 Jan 2026

### EU bans fuels refined from Russian crude

The EU’s 18th sanctions package takes effect: diesel and jet fuel made from Russian crude in other countries, mainly India and Turkey, can no longer be sold into the EU. Jamnagar’s export flows to Europe shrink.

Show on the globe ↑
24. 28 Feb 2026

### War with Iran closes Hormuz

US and Israeli strikes on Iran begin on 28 February. Iran restricts the Strait, lays mines and attacks merchant ships. Roughly a fifth of world oil and LNG supply is stranded. Brent climbs from about $72 to nearly $120, a 51% jump in March alone.

Show on the globe ↑
25. 5 Mar 2026

### Qatar LNG halted; Gulf energy sites hit

Attacks hit energy facilities across the Gulf. A strike on Ras Laffan knocks out about 17% of Qatar’s LNG capacity, which will take around three years to repair. With Hormuz shut, Qatar exports almost nothing; Asian and European gas prices surge and the US becomes the world’s marginal LNG supplier.

Show on the globe ↑
26. 15 Mar 2026

### Bypass pipelines & emergency stocks

Saudi Arabia runs the East–West pipeline to Yanbu near its about 7 mb/d limit and the UAE maxes out its Habshan–Fujairah line. Even together they replace only about a third of the lost Gulf exports. The IEA coordinates a 400-million-barrel stock release. Asian buyers bid up US, West African and Brazilian crude.

Show on the globe ↑
27. 7 Apr 2026

### A ceasefire, then a US blockade of Iran

A two-week ceasefire is agreed on 7 April, but the Strait remains contested. From 13 April to 29 May the US Navy blockades Iranian ports, choking off Iran’s exports to China.

Show on the globe ↑
28. 15 Jun 2026

### A deal to reopen Hormuz, with tolls

A June memorandum aims to reopen the Strait, but Iran seeks control of the waterway and moves to charge passing ships. Periodic Iranian attacks and retaliatory US strikes keep traffic well below normal through the summer.

Show on the globe ↑
29. 28 Sep 2026

### Crude recovers, fuel stays scarce

Tanker tracking shows Hormuz crude flows back near pre-war levels as backlogged cargoes finally sail. Fuel shipments are a different story: still about 0.7 mb/d against 3.6 mb/d before the war. Fuel-importing nations such as the Philippines and Australia face shortages and rationing.

Show on the globe ↑
30. 23 Oct 2026

### Houthis strike Saudi Arabia; Brent $117

Houthi strikes on Saudi energy sites send Brent to $116.72, its highest close since April, and put the Yanbu bypass at risk. Hormuz oil transits sit around 80% of pre-war levels. Qatar, unable to ship, signs a 4 Mt/yr deal to buy US LNG from Cheniere through 2054. The map has been redrawn.

### Europe swapped pipes for ships

Before the invasion, about 150 billion cubic metres of Russian gas a year reached the EU through four pipeline routes. By 2025 only one, TurkStream, was still running. Norway's pipelines and a steady stream of LNG tankers from the US Gulf Coast filled the gap. Gas that used to come straight from Siberia now crosses the Atlantic.

### Russian oil didn't disappear, it just travels further

The EU embargo and the $60 price cap in December 2022 didn't take Russian crude off the market. They made the trip longer. Cargoes that used to reach Rotterdam in a few days now spend about a month sailing through Suez to India. For a while Indian refineries even sold diesel made from that oil back to Europe, until the EU closed the loophole in January 2026.

### A single strait can reroute the world

Within a few weeks of the Houthi attacks in late 2023, most tankers going from the Gulf to Europe were sailing around Africa instead. Then in 2026 the war with Iran hit the one chokepoint with no real way around it. Saudi Arabia and the UAE have bypass pipelines, but together they could carry only about a third of what normally goes through Hormuz, and Qatar's LNG had no other way out at all.

### The Americas picked up the slack

US oil and LNG exports set new records almost every year. Guyana and Brazil grew fast, and Canada finally got a pipeline to the Pacific. After the US captured Maduro in January 2026, Venezuelan oil stopped going to small Chinese refiners and started heading to the US. By late 2026 even Qatar had signed up to buy American LNG.

## How I made it, and what to take with a grain of salt

The routes are drawn by hand. Shipping lanes follow the usual tanker paths through the real straits, and pipelines follow their rough corridors. For each route I set volumes at key dates and the map fills in the months between. The numbers come from the IEA, EIA, Bruegel, news coverage of Kpler and Vortexa tanker data, and company reports. I left out small flows and anything moving within a single country. The 2026 numbers are from news reports at the time, so expect them to be revised. Please don't trade on any of this.
