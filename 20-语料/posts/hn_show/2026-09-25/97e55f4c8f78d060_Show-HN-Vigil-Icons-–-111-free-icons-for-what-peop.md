Why these exist
I went looking for these. They didn’t exist.
Every icon set on earth has a house, a gear, a magnifying glass, a heart. Ten thousand ways to say “settings.” Zero ways to say gaslighting — the word half the internet used this week.
Icons are supposed to be the fastest way to say something. So why do they only say the boring things? The vocabulary people actually reach for is behavioral, judgmental, a little mean, and very funny. Nobody had drawn it.
So we drew it. Stroke-first and clean, because the joke should look like it belongs in a system. Slightly irreverent, because the phrases are. And free, because a punchline behind a paywall isn’t a punchline.

The object is recognizable. Then it misbehaves.
01 · Phrase People actually say it. Out loud, in a group chat, about someone. If it needs a caption, it isn’t a Vigil icon.
02 · Accusation It describes a person or a moment and can be sent pointedly. “I need to send this to somebody” is the product.
03 · The object does the joke A backwards flag. A house inside a head. A tipped container. Ordinary object, socially revealing violation. You notice it behaving in a way it absolutely should not.
Above 40, nearly every concept has all three. From 41 down, one of them starts needing help — and slang gets discounted hard. Generational plain labels (Boomer, Millennial, Gen Z, Gen X, Gen Alpha) are out entirely: taxonomy, not jokes. “Couch stuck on stairs” is a better icon than “glowing rectangle.”

Questions, answered.
Is Vigil Icons free?
 Yes. All 111 icons, every format, no email, no account. The font is free, and the icons only work when they get sent — a paywall was 91 jokes nobody would paste into a group chat. If they earn you something, buy Keno a pint .
Can I use Vigil Icons commercially?
 Yes. Personal and commercial use are both fine: apps, sites, decks, stickers, merch, client work, unlimited projects, no attribution required. Released under the SIL Open Font License 1.1 — the same license as the Vigil typeface. Use them in anything, including things you sell. The one no is selling the icons on their own. Redraws are welcome but stay under this license and can’t carry the Vigil name. Full terms: LICENSE.txt .
What formats do Vigil Icons come in?
 SVG on a 24 grid in currentColor, one path per icon; PNG at 256 black on white and 512 transparent; 128px emoji for Slack and Discord; sticker packs for iMessage, Telegram and WhatsApp; an npm package with React components and a web component; and icons.json with every name and line.
How do I use Vigil Icons in code?
 Run npm install vigil-icons and import one component per icon, or add the web component with a single script tag from vigilicons.com/icons/vigil-icon.js. Plain SVGs live at vigilicons.com/icons/svg/ and in the sprite at /icons/sprite.svg.
How do I add Vigil Icons to Slack, Discord, iMessage, WhatsApp or Telegram?
 Download the emoji or sticker pack from the Downloads page. Slack: Customize workspace → Emoji → Add. Discord: Server settings → Emoji → Upload. iMessage: save the PNGs to Photos and send them from the photo picker. Telegram: send the PNGs to @Stickers. WhatsApp: import the WebP folder with a sticker-maker app.
Do I get future icons?
 Yes. Every new Vigil icon lands on this page, in the zips and in the npm package the day it ships. Nothing to buy, nothing to renew.
Can I request an icon?
 Yes — use the request form under Drops. Vote on what’s drawn next or pitch a phrase; new concepts welcome if they pass the rule: phrase → accusation → the object does the joke.
Who makes Vigil Icons, and where do I get support?
 Keno Vigil, an artist and technologist in Cary, North Carolina, who drew all 111 by hand, made the free Vigil typeface they are set in, and wrote the code that ships them. Support is through thinkkeno.com/#contact ; one person answers, usually within a day.
