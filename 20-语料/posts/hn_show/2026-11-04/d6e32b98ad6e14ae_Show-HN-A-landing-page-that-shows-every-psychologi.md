FAQ PART:
Acknowledging Resistance
READ BEFORE YOU BUY
01
 What do I get inside?
 +
You get access to Brainiac and Unicorn Warrior.
Brainiac is the psychology vault: 150+ cognitive biases, behavioral models, principles, guides, research, examples, stats, and flashcards.
Unicorn Warrior is the case study vault: 230+ creative viral marketing case studies broken down into timelines, strategies, psychology, and windows of opportunity.
02
 Is this based on real research?
 +
Yes.
Brainiac is built from scientific papers, behavioral science books, classic psychology concepts, real marketing campaigns, and business examples.
The goal is to make useful research readable without forcing you to dig through 47-page PDFs written by people allergic to normal sentences.
03
 How is this different from reading psychology books?
 +
Books usually explain the idea, often using storytelling, like A LOT.
A 230-page book can introduce you to up to 10 principles without any guidance on how to use it in your business.
Books you use when you want to go deeper. You use Brainiac when you want to make it useful.
Authenticity Effect
04
 How is this different from AI?
 +
I really, really wish AI could solve this by itself. That would save me months.
But if you’ve used AI for very niche problems, you’ve probably noticed that it can give you kinda generic/obvious ideas, or full-blown nonsense. And it does it with great confidence.
Don’t get me wrong. AI is great. You can use it instead of this website.
This website is different because it’s a shortcut to 5 years of my work, thousands of hours of digging, writing, testing, collecting, and organizing.
You get a structured vault of specific principles, research-backed effects, practical examples, stats, guides, and case studies you can browse when you’re working on a real business problem.
Also, this vault has taste.
ChatGPT sometimes says things like “leverage emotional resonance.”
Nobody needs that in their life.
05
 Will this work for my industry?
 +
Most likely, yes.
The principles are universal, not specialized to one market.
They can apply to SaaS, ecommerce, agencies, info products, apps, newsletters, marketplaces, communities, coaching, services, restaurants, events, creators, and strange little internet businesses that somehow make money.
06
 Do I need to know psychology already?
 +
No.
Everything is written in simple language. You don’t need academic background, behavioral science vocabulary, or a PhD in making people click buttons.
07
 Is this about manipulation?
 +
No.
It’s about understanding how people make decisions, what makes them hesitate, what makes them trust, and what helps them act.
You can use psychology to make things clearer, easier, safer, and more desirable.
Don’t use it like a creep.
08
 Will this make me a better marketer?
 +
If you actually use it, yes.
You’ll start seeing patterns behind good marketing instead of just staring at nice landing pages and thinking, “hmm, looks expensive.”
You’ll understand why some offers feel obvious, why some products spread, why some pages convert, and why some campaigns get ignored even when they look “expensive.”
09
 Will this make me more creative?
 +
Probably.
Not in the “light a candle, speak 7 affirmations, and wait for inspiration” way.
More in the “I now have 200+ weirdest marketing campaigns in history that made a bank, and I can combine them into better ideas” way.
Good creativity usually comes from having more useful material to combine.

If you already have taste and experience, this gives you more raw material.

Authenticity Effect + Pratfall Effect
15
 What’s wrong with this website?
 +
Honestly, a few things.
This website is over 5 years old. When I started building it, I knew almost nothing about programming. I was learning by breaking things, fixing them, breaking them again, and pretending that was a process.
Because of that, some older parts don’t feel as polished as the newer ones. Some pages have design decisions I probably wouldn’t make today. Some sections are dense. Some layouts are a bit weird. Some parts still feel like they were built by someone trying to duct-tape a big idea into WordPress.
That someone was me.
Same with the case studies.
There are cases from the very beginning that I’m honestly a little embarrassed by now. Not because I didn’t care. I cared too much. I spent 15 to 30+ hours on every case from day one.
But my knowledge was smaller back then. I didn’t understand marketing mechanics, psychology, product growth, positioning, timing, and distribution as deeply as I do now. Some older breakdowns are useful, but they don’t always match the level of the newer ones.
So yes, the archive has uneven parts.
I’m slowly improving them, rewriting old case studies, cleaning up weak sections, and making the whole thing better piece by piece.
The good part is that this product has 5+ years of work inside it.
The honest part is that you can sometimes see those 5 years.
