 How a run works
 One move per week. Each one costs energy, and energy is the only thing you can't buy.
 Build
 Push the product forward. At 20% it goes live. Every week of code is worth a little less than the last.
Build in public
 Post about what you're making. Followers today, signups once the product is live.
Talk to users
 Calls with real people. More conversions, less churn and a list of tiny missing features.
Launch
 A spike of signups. Once every six weeks, and each launch works a bit less than the one before.
Freelance
 €1,400 in the bank and a week not spent on your product. Sometimes it's the right call.
Unplug
 A week offline to get your energy back. Below 30 every move yields less. At zero it's burnout.
Two finish lines
 €1,500 MRR
 Ramen profitable
 Enough for rent and ramen. The first milestone that keeps you out of an office.

 At week 52, not before. Getting there early doesn't end the run: you can still burn out or slip back.
Things happen
 A VC offers you money. A funded competitor shows up. Product Hunt, viral threads, churn waves, acquisition offers, and your mother calling to ask how it's going. Every week brings an event, and every answer has a cost.

 From the exit to burnout, with a few surprises in between. The ones you haven't seen stay a mystery until you get there.

Questions
 What is Ramen Profitable?
 + A free narrative game that runs in your browser. You play a solo founder who quits their job to build a SaaS, with 52 in-game weeks to reach €10,000 in monthly recurring revenue (MRR). It's made by Pierquinto Manco, an indie hacker from Milan.
 What does "ramen profitable" mean?
 + It's a term coined by Paul Graham in 2009: a startup that makes just enough money to pay the founders' living expenses, ramen included. In the game the threshold is €1,500 of MRR.
 Is it free? Do I need an account?
 + It's free and you can play as a guest. A free account, with a sign-in link sent by email and no password, saves your endings, puts you on the leaderboard and unlocks custom ideas.
 Can I play with my own startup idea?
 + Yes. Describe it in a sentence and the game turns it into a playable SaaS, with its own price, audience and churn. Custom ideas have their own leaderboard.
 How realistic is the simulation?
 + It's a game, not a financial model. But the mechanics come from real indie hacking: churn that eats your growth, launches with diminishing returns, conversions that improve when you talk to users, runway that runs out, and energy that runs out first.
 Does it work on mobile?
 + Yes, it runs in any modern browser, on desktop and on mobile. The room is drawn in 3D with WebGL, so an older phone may struggle a little.
 Which languages is it in?
 + English and Italian. You can switch from the game's title screen or from the settings.
