### The app

 How is Vitals different from Activity Monitor?

Activity Monitor lists processes, about a thousand on a working Mac. Vitals lists apps. Every helper is added to the app that launched it, so each app has one figure for memory, CPU, energy, disk and network. Vitals also keeps 30 days of history, sends a notification when an app misbehaves, lives in the menu bar, and finds dev servers left running.

 Does it replace Activity Monitor completely?

For everyday questions, yes: what is using the memory, what is draining the battery, what is slowing the Mac down. You can quit or force quit an app or a single process from Vitals. Activity Monitor still has a few specialist tools, such as sampling a process, that Vitals does not copy.

 Does it slow my Mac down?

With no window open it samples every five seconds and uses about a tenth of a percent of one core. It samples faster only while you are looking at it.

 Which Macs does it run on?

macOS 15 Sequoia or later, on Apple silicon and Intel. Power and temperature figures are most detailed on Apple silicon.

 Why is it not on the Mac App Store?

The App Store sandbox stops an app from reading other apps' processes, which is the one thing an activity monitor has to do. Vitals is signed with our Apple Developer ID and notarized by Apple, so it opens like any other app.

### Privacy and safety

 Does it send anything about my Mac anywhere?

Nothing about what you run. Your apps, your history and your project names stay on the Mac. Vitals goes online only to check for updates and to check your license. Activating sends the key, the Mac's name and an anonymous ID, so you can tell your Macs apart when you move a license.

 What is the optional helper?

macOS only lets an app read your own processes. To include the system's, you can enable a small helper. It answers one question, how much these processes are using, takes no commands, changes nothing, and quits when Vitals closes. Vitals works without it.

 Can Vitals quit apps on its own?

No. It only quits an app, a process or a dev server when you ask, and it shows a confirmation first.
