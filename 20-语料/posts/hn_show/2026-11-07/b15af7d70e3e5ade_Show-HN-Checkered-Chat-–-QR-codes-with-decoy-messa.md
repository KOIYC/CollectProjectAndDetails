So what do you actually make with one?
Fair question. “QR code messenger” doesn’t answer it.
Scavenger hunts
Print a sheet of stickers, a clue in each one, and hide them around a park or an office. Put a riddle on the last code so nobody can skip to the end.
Cards worth opening twice
Two notes in one code. The table scans “Happy birthday” off their own phones; the person it’s for gets the longer version.
Group gifts
Split the note across five codes and pick the arithmetic — any three open it, any two open nothing. Everyone has to turn up.
Notes in a lunchbox
Or a jacket pocket, or taped inside a book. It’s a picture, so it goes wherever paper goes.
Escape rooms and pop-ups
A code on a table tent that isn’t a link, can’t redirect anybody, and works with the wifi down.
The long one, across a room
Too much text for a single code becomes a set of frames the other phone catches in any order. No pairing, no network, nothing to join.

Five ways to say it. One way to show it.
Open and Locked are free. Decoy, Split and Animated are the unlock, and your
 first three are on the house.
Open
Ordinary text in an ordinary code. Anyone’s camera reads it, which is sometimes exactly what you want.
Locked
A question only they can answer. “What did we have for breakfast?” — capitals and punctuation don’t matter, because nobody remembers their own punctuation.
Decoy
Two notes in one code. Any other camera reads the first; Checkered Chat reads both. It’s the birthday card that scans as “Happy birthday” for the whole table and as something better for the person it’s for.
Split
One message across several codes, and you choose the arithmetic: any three of five open it, any two tell you nothing at all. Not less — nothing.
Animated
A message too long for one code, played as a set of frames the other phone catches in any order, with a ring that fills as they arrive.
Show mode
Full screen, full brightness, and a timer: show a code for five seconds and it clears itself. Turn the phone over and it’s gone.

Privacy Policy
Last updated September 29, 2026
Checkered Chat collects nothing, sends nothing, and stores nothing about you. This is not a promise about careful handling. There is no data, and there is no networking code in the app to send it with.
What leaves your device
Nothing. There are no analytics, no crash reporting, no advertising identifiers, no accounts and no server. Turn on airplane mode and the app behaves identically, because it never had anything to say to the network in the first place.
The only outward links are the ones in About — this page, support and terms — which open in your browser when you tap them. Tapping one leaves the app; nothing about you goes with it.
The camera
Checkered Chat asks for camera access because scanning a code is half the app. Frames are examined on the device, looking only for QR codes. Nothing is recorded, photographed, saved or transmitted.
Your photos
Importing a picture of a code uses the system picker, which hands the app the one picture you chose and nothing else — the app has no access to your library and never asks for it.
Saving a code to your photos asks for add-only permission the first time, which lets the app put a picture in and gives it no ability to read anything already there.
On your watch
If you send a code to an Apple Watch, the watch can tell you it arrived. That notification says “A code arrived” and nothing else — never the message, never a preview, not even the first note of a decoy. A lock screen is the most public surface you own, and a message you had to make a code for does not belong on it. Reading it still means opening the app.
What is written to disk
Only settings, never messages. Whether you have seen the introduction. The printer alignment nudges you save on the sticker sheet screen, kept per sheet of stock so you do not have to find them twice. Whether you have bought the unlock, and how many of the three free codes you have used. And the mode and options you last chose in the Messages app, so it opens where you left it.
The look you last chose, too: module shape, the two colors, whether there is a center mark, how fast an animated set plays, and the size you are printing at. A house color you use every day should not have to be picked every day. What you wrote is not among them — not the message, not either half of a decoy, not a lock’s question. The app remembers that you like round modules in navy. It does not remember a word you put in one.
The last three are shared between the app and its Messages extension, because they are two separate programs that have to agree — otherwise the three free codes would be three in each, and buying the unlock in one would not unlock the other. Nothing you write or scan is among them. The Messages extension remembers that you last chose Decoy; it does not remember a single word of what you wrote.
Messages are never written down. What you write exists while you are writing it. What you scan exists while you are reading it, and is gone when you close it — there is no history, no inbox and no way to ask the app what it saw yesterday, because it did not keep it.
Your purchase is recorded by the App Store, as every purchase is. The app asks Apple what you own; it does not keep a record of its own and has no receipt server to check with.
Children
Checkered Chat collects no personal information from anyone, of any age. There is no mechanism by which it could, so there is nothing collected from a child either — no accounts, no sign-up, no directory, and no way for a stranger to reach anyone through the app. A code arrives because somebody handed it over.
The App Store listing is rated 4+, which is what Apple's age questionnaire produces for it: there is no objectionable content in the app, and nothing in it but what its user wrote.
Changes
If any of this ever stops being true, this page changes first and the app's version notes will say so plainly. The date at the top is when this page last changed.
Who to ask

A message only one person can read is a fine thing between friends and a poor thing to hand around a school; section 7 asks you not to use it that way, and that request is the part that matters.
3. Not a security product
Checkered Chat is a toy. It is not encrypted messaging, not a password manager, and not a means of protecting sensitive information. A locked code can be guessed at indefinitely by whoever holds it. A decoy hides a message from a casual glance, not from anyone who examines what the code actually contains. Do not use any of it for something whose disclosure would matter.
4. Cryptography
Locked codes are closed using cryptography built into Apple's own operating systems — AES-GCM and PBKDF2, reached through Apple's CryptoKit and CommonCrypto frameworks. Checkered Chat writes none of its own, with one exception: the arithmetic that splits a key across several codes, which is Shamir's Secret Sharing — a scheme published in 1979 and implemented here from the published description, not invented for this app. None of this makes the app a security product; section 3 above is the plain truth and this section does not soften it.
Some countries restrict the import, use or export of software that performs cryptography. The app does not know or ask where you are. Using it in accordance with the law where you are is your responsibility.
5. Nothing is recoverable
The app keeps no copy of anything you write or scan. If you lose a code, the message in it is gone, and we cannot retrieve it — not as a policy, but because it was never anywhere but in your hands.
6. Screenshots and photographs
A code on a screen can be photographed, and a printed code can be picked up. The app tells you when a screenshot is taken while a code is showing; it cannot prevent one, and does not claim to.
7. Acceptable use
Checkered Chat is for notes between people who want to exchange them. Do not use it to harass, intimidate, frighten or follow anyone. Do not send codes to someone who has asked you to stop, and do not leave them where they are likely to be found by a person who would rather not have found them. Do not use it to break the law, or to carry anything it is illegal to hold or to pass on.
One thing deserves saying on its own, because it is the only misuse this app makes easier than it would otherwise be. A decoy exists so a message looks dull to everyone except its reader. It is not there to trick somebody into scanning something they would not have scanned, and it is not there to carry content past a person or a process whose job is to look at it. That is not what a decoy is for.
There is no report button, and the reason is worth saying plainly rather than hiding. Nothing you write reaches us: no server, no account, no copy. So there is nobody here who could read a reported code, and a button leading nowhere would be worse than no button. If somebody is using a code against you, the code itself is the evidence — keep it. Take it to whoever carried it: the platform the message arrived on, your school or workplace, or the police. A code inserted in Messages sits in a conversation Apple’s own reporting already covers.
What you write, who you hand it to, and where you leave it are yours.

Support
Questions, bugs, a scanner that will not read a printed code, or an idea worth building — write to support@honeywired.com .
Two things help enormously: what the code was made in (Open, Locked, Decoy, Split or Animated), and whether it was on a screen or on paper. Printed problems are usually size problems, and the app will tell you which size would have worked.
Questions we expect
Do other people need the app to read my code?
 Not for an Open code — any camera reads it, because it is ordinary text in an ordinary QR code. Locked, Decoy, Split and Animated codes need Checkered Chat, because there is something to unlock, uncover or reassemble.
What is free and what costs?
 Reading is always free. Scanning any code costs nothing, including a decoy somebody hands you, and so does writing an open or locked message and showing it on your screen.
Making decoys, split sets and animated sets, and printing sticker sheets, are the one-time unlock. Your first three codes in a paid mode are free — three in total, spent on whichever modes you like — so you can see what one is like before deciding.
Can a code somebody hands me do something to my phone?
 Not one of ours. A Checkered Chat code carries text and nothing else — never a web address, never anything your phone could be talked into opening. That is a rule the app is built on rather than a setting you can turn off, and it is why a code from a stranger is safe to point a camera at.
This is worth knowing because it is not true of QR codes in general. Most codes on posters and menus are links, and a link can go anywhere. Ours are not links, so there is nowhere for one to take you.
How safe is a locked code?
 It is a bit of fun, not a safe. Anyone holding the code can keep guessing the answer for as long as they like, and we would rather say so than let you find out later. Do not use it for anything whose disclosure would matter.
I scanned something and now I cannot find it.
 That is the app working. Messages are not kept — there is no history and no inbox, and nothing to recover. If you need to keep something, save the code itself as a picture before you close it.
My printed stickers will not scan.
 Almost always one of two things. Either the code is too dense for the size it was printed at — the app grades this before you print, and names the size that would work — or the sheet was printed with “fit to page” on, which shrinks everything and moves every label off the stock. Print at 100%.
The labels are slightly out of line with my sheet.
 Printers drift. The sticker sheet screen can print an alignment check — outlines only — which you hold against a real sheet, and then nudge the layout in half-millimeter steps until they sit on the labels.
I paid, and a feature is locked again.
 Open About and tap Restore purchases. That asks the App Store what your Apple Account owns. If it still says nothing, check that you are signed in with the account you bought it on.
Can I get a refund?
 Yes, but not from us — Apple handles every App Store purchase and refund, and we never see the transaction. Go to reportaproblem.apple.com , sign in, and pick the purchase. If something about the app is the reason, we would like to hear it either way.
What age is this for?
 The listing is rated 4+, which is what the App Store's age questionnaire produces for it: there is nothing objectionable in the app, no accounts, no strangers and no content but your own. That is a statement about what is inside it, not a recommendation. A message only one person can read is a fine thing between friends and a poor thing to hand around a school, so: please do not use it to make somebody's life harder. That request does more work than a number would.
