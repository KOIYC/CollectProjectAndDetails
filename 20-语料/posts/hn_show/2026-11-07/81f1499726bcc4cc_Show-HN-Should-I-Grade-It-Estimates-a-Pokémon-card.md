How much does PSA grading cost in 2026?
PSA's cheapest tier is Value again: $39.99 a card from October 29, 2026, for cards worth up to $500. Standard is $59.99 for cards up to $1,000 and Priority $79.99 up to $1,500. With shipping and insurance, one card costs roughly $60 to $75 through Value and $80 to $95 through Standard.
PSA paused its cheaper tiers on June 2 to work through a backlog, added Standard in September, and on October 28 announced Value's return and cut the Standard and Priority turnaround estimates by 20 business days. New Value orders join the queue behind everything already at PSA. Value Bulk stays paused until the backlog, 7.2 million cards on October 20, falls to about 5 million.

Is my Pokémon card worth grading?
It's worth grading when the card's value at the grade it will likely get, minus its raw value, clearly beats the cost of grading, and still doesn't lose money one grade lower. With PSA's Value tier back, that means clearing roughly $65 per card for cards worth up to $500 graded, or $85 through Standard for cards up to $1,000, before any selling fees.
Only if the grade adds clearly more than it costs, since a slab can come back lower than you hoped. Here's the Lugia at today's PSA pricing:

What centering does a PSA 10 need?
About 55/45 or better on the front, both left to right and top to bottom, and 75/25 or better on the back. A PSA 9 allows about 60/40 on the front. Corners, edges and surface also need to be flawless for a 10.
Centering is the first thing to check, because it's the easiest to measure and it can't be fixed. PSA's limits:
Grade Front Back
PSA 10 55/45 75/25
PSA 9 60/40 90/10
The app measures centering from your front photo to the percent, so you know right away whether a 10 is even possible. Check yours with the centering calculator , or read how to measure it yourself .
How to photograph Pokémon cards for an accurate scan
One light source, like a lamp or a bright window.
Phone straight above the card, card filling most of the screen.
For the tilted shot, tip the card until the glare slides across it. Scratches light up; printing doesn't.
Keep the whole card in the frame and sharp: the card is identified from the front photo itself.

FAQ
Is this an official grade?
 No. It's an estimate from photos, to help you pick which cards are worth submitting. Only graders like PSA, CGC and Beckett can give an official grade.
PSA or another grader, which should I use?
 PSA slabs usually sell for the most, which matters for high-value cards you plan to sell. CGC and Beckett are alternatives that can cost less per card. This app estimates the PSA grade, with subgrades showing what holds each card back.
What's the cheapest way to get a card graded right now?
 PSA's Value tier, back from October 29, 2026 at $39.99 per card for cards worth up to $500, with an estimated 90 to 100 business days. New Value orders queue behind everything already at PSA, so Standard at $59.99, now 70 to 80 business days, can come back sooner. CGC and Beckett can cost less per card too.
Can I just point my phone at the card?
 Yes. Tap Live scan and point your camera at the card. It takes the front photo itself once the picture is sharp, then reads the card number live as you bring it into the box, and locks on once several readings agree. Free accounts get 7 full scans a month, and Pro is unlimited.
Does it work on One Piece cards?
 The free tools do. The centering calculator and the grading calculator work for One Piece, Magic, sports cards or anything else with a border, by typing in the border widths or the prices. The photo scanner and the price guide are Pokémon only for now, because that's the catalog it's matched against.
Does it work on promos and newer sets?
 Yes. It IDs English Pokémon cards from the collector number, including promos like SWSH283 and Trainer Gallery cards like TG14/TG30.
Where do the prices come from?
 Raw is TCGplayer's market price for that exact printing, refreshed daily. Graded is the median of recent eBay sales at that grade, with links to the latest sold listings. Every result links to the card's TCGplayer page.
Is this connected to TCGplayer?
 It uses TCGplayer's public catalog and market prices, so card names, numbers, printings and prices match what you'd see on TCGplayer. It isn't run by or affiliated with TCGplayer.

 On your device straight away, and with a free account, to your account too. Sign in with your email on any phone or computer and your collection comes back, with each card's value tracked daily from TCGplayer and your total collection value charted over time. How does it recognise the card?
 The front photo is sent to CardSight AI, a card recognition service, which names the card. The app then matches it to the exact TCGplayer printing for prices. The grading measurements, centering, corners, edges and surface, are done on your phone. Add the app to your home screen and it opens like an app.
What about Beckett, CGC and TAG grades? Beckett's 10 is Pristine and its Gem Mint is a 9.5; a Black Label is a Pristine 10 with all four subgrades at 10. CGC, TAG and SGC each have a Gem Mint 10 and a stricter Pristine 10. A Black Label can sell for several times a PSA 10, but a BGS 9.5 sells for well under one. The full comparison is here.

Grading costs roughly $60 to $95 a card once shipping is included, takes months, and plenty of cards come back worth less than that.Should I Grade It? takes a photo of a card, identifies it, estimates the PSA grade, and pulls the raw price from TCGplayer plus recent eBay sales at each grade. Then it does the sum using the PSA tier the card's value requires, since a $5,000 card can't go through a tier capped at $500.How it works:The measurement runs client-side with canvas: find the card, warp it flat, measure the borders for centering (PSA publishes the limits, 55/45 for a 10), and check the back for corner and edge whitening. An optional tilted photo is used to look for surface scratches.The front photo goes through a Cloudflare Worker to CardSight AI for identification. Prices come from TCGCSV, a mirror of TCGplayer's catalog, and graded sales from PokemonPriceTracker. The app is a single HTML file with no framework; the Worker also renders about 25,000 card price pages from KV.There's an inspector where you can drag the measuring lines yourself if you don't trust the automatic ones.Limitations: it's an estimate from photos, not a grade. Centering works best on yellow-bordered cards; full-art cards with thin silver borders often can't be measured automatically, and it says so rather than guessing. Corner sharpness can't really be judged from a flat photo, only whitening.Free use is 7 scans a month, and there's a paid tier for unlimited scans. The centering tool and grading calculator are free with no limit.I'd especially like feedback on the measurement approach (glare and perspective are the hard parts), and from anyone who has sent cards to PSA on whether the estimates match what came back.
