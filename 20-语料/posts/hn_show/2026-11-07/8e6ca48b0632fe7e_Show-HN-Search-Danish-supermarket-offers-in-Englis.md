# Supermarket Offers in Denmark

Daily grocery prices at Rema 1000, Netto, Føtex, Lidl, Bilka, 365 Discount, Spar and Meny.

Rema 1000 Føtex Netto Lidl Bilka 365 Discount Spar Meny

Compare supermarket offers across Denmark

Find the best supermarket offers in Denmark in one place. This page collects the weekly grocery offers (tilbud) from eight Danish supermarkets: Rema 1000, Netto, Føtex, Lidl, Bilka, 365 Discount, Spar and Meny. Instead of going through eight different tilbudsavis leaflets, search for one product and see which shop has the lowest offer price right now.

The price compare tool works in English, so you don’t need to read Danish. Search for “eggs”, “coffee” or “chicken” and it finds the Danish product names for you. Prices are updated every night.

How to find the cheapest groceries in Denmark

- Compare the unit price, not the pack price. Look at kr./kg, kr./l or kr./stk. A bigger pack on offer is not always the better deal.
- Check offers before you shop. Each supermarket starts its new weekly offers on its own day, so the cheapest shop can change in the middle of the week.
- Don’t stick to one shop. Discount chains like Netto, Rema 1000, Lidl and 365 Discount are known as the budget option, but Føtex, Bilka or Meny can have the best price on a single product in a given week.
- Stock up when staples are on offer. Coffee, rice, pasta, tinned food and frozen meat keep well, so buy extra when the price drops.

Frequently asked questions

How to use the supermarket offer finder

Search for a product like “egg”, “milk” or “chicken”. You don’t need to know the Danish word: the tool translates common grocery words for you. You will see the best deal at the top, and below it every matching offer from each shop. When pack sizes are different, the tool compares the price per kilo, litre or piece, so a big pack and a small pack are compared fairly.

Want to see everything on sale at one shop? Open the “This week’s offers” tab, pick Rema 1000, Netto, Føtex, Lidl, Bilka, 365 Discount, Spar or Meny, and sort by lowest price or biggest discount.

Which supermarket is cheapest in Denmark?

It changes from week to week and from product to product. One shop can have the cheapest eggs this week and another the cheapest coffee. That is why this tool compares the current offers instead of naming one “cheapest” supermarket.

What does “tilbud” mean?

“Tilbud” is Danish for offer or deal. A “tilbudsavis” is the weekly leaflet where each supermarket lists its offers. The prices in this tool come from those weekly offers.

How often are the prices updated?

Every night. Most Danish supermarkets start new offers during the week, so check again before you go shopping. The shop’s own leaflet always applies if a price is different.

Why does a shop say “No offer this week”?

It means the product is not on offer at that shop right now. The shop may still sell it at its normal price. This tool only shows offer prices, not everyday shelf prices.

What does kr./kg or kr./stk mean?

That is the unit price: kroner per kilo (kg), per litre (l) or per piece (stk, short for “styk”). Use it to compare different pack sizes.

Useful Danish grocery words

- Æg = eggs
- Mælk = milk
- Smør = butter
- Brød = bread
- Kylling = chicken
- Hakket oksekød = minced beef
- Ris = rice
- Økologisk = organic
- Tilbud = offer
- Kampagnepris = promotion price
