ImmoLens – KI-gestützte Immobilienanalyse
ImmoLens analysiert Immobilien-Exposés mit künstlicher Intelligenz: versteckte Kosten erkennen, Sanierungsbedarf bewerten und KfW/BAFA/L-Bank Förderungen optimieren.
So funktioniert's
Exposé hochladen – PDF, Screenshots oder Text des Inserats einfügen (oder per Chrome-Erweiterung mit einem Klick importieren)
KI analysiert – Unsere KI erkennt versteckte Kosten, Sanierungsbedarf und Risiken
Förderungen finden – Automatische Prüfung auf KfW, BAFA und L-Bank Programme
Fundiert entscheiden – Mit allen Fakten sicher verhandeln und kaufen
Features
Automatische KI-Analyse von Exposés und Energieausweisen
Förder-Optimierung: KfW 124, KfW 358, KfW 261, BAFA, L-Bank
Finanzierungsrechner mit Budget-Stresstest
Risiko-Check für versteckte Kosten
Interaktive Kartenansicht aller analysierten Immobilien
Verhandlungsassistent mit KI-gestützten Preisempfehlungen
Häufig gestellte Fragen
Ist ImmoLens kostenlos?
Ja! die Basisversion mit KI-Analyse und Förder-Check ist dauerhaft kostenlos. Für Premium-Features wie unbegrenzte Projekte und Verhandlungsassistent gibt es Plus- und Pro-Abos.
Wie genau ist die KI-Analyse?
ImmoLens nutzt Google Gemini in der aktuellen Modellgeneration und gleicht die Ergebnisse mit aktuellen Marktdaten ab. Die Analyse wird durch strukturierte Datenextraktion ergänzt.
Sind meine Daten sicher?
Ja. Alle hochgeladenen Dateien werden verschlüsselt gespeichert (AES-256). Zur KI-Analyse werden Inhalte erst nach automatischer Entfernung erkennbarer Kontaktdaten an die Google Gemini API übergeben. Details stehen in der Datenschutzerklärung.

> **martinvoelker** · 2026-07-31T12:45:59.000Z　
> Hi HN, I am Martin, an AI engineer from Germany. ImmoLens is my nights-and-weekends project: an AI that analyzes German real estate listings from the buyer's side. The link opens the English UI directly (the site is German-first otherwise) and the analysis itself can be produced in English too, so you can actually try it. The English support is not an afterthought: a lot of buyers in Germany never grew up with the rules here (expats, international hires, US military families stationed near bases like Ramstein or Stuttgart), and German subsidy bureaucracy definitely does not translate itself.
> The problem: When you buy a house in Germany, everyone at the table profits from a higher price: the agent (commission), the seller, often even the bank. The buyer is alone with a glossy PDF full of phrases like "lovingly renovated" (translation: new paint, original 1970s heating). A proper surveyor costs 1,500€+ per property, which nobody pays at the shortlist stage when you are still comparing ten listings.What it does: You upload a listing (PDF, screenshot, or photo of a printout). The AI extracts the property data and gives you: estimated renovation costs by trade (roof, heating, windows...), red flags in the listing language, and which government subsidies match the property. The subsidy part is the unfair advantage: Germany has federal programs (KfW, BAFA) plus 16 state-level development banks with subsidized loans up to 350,000€, and virtually no buyer knows they exist. The spread is absurd: the same family gets 350k€ in subsidized loans in one state and 180k€ in another.Tech: Express monolith, React SPA, Gemini for the analysis pipeline, Postgres on Railway, plus a Capacitor-wrapped Android app that shipped to the Play Store this month. The interesting part was prompt-engineering the cost estimation to stay honest: the model outputs ranges with explicit confidence and cites which listing detail triggered which estimate, instead of hallucinating precise numbers. There is also a non-AI part I enjoyed building: a neighborhood score (0-100) for any German address, computed from OpenStreetMap via Overpass (grocery, transit, schools, healthcare within walking distance), with a geo-grid cache so I stay a polite Overpass citizen. Newest addition: a viewing-appointment mode on mobile, a 45-point checklist plus camera that files photos and a summary straight into the property's diary while you walk through the house.A constraint that turned into a feature: German portals forbid automated access to their listings, so ImmoLens never fetches a listing URL on the server. Everything runs on what the user hands over: a PDF, a screenshot, a photo of a printout, or a one-click import that runs inside the user's own browser. Designing around that turned out to make the product better, not worse: photographing a paper exposé during a viewing became one of the most-used entry points.Honest limitations: It is German-market only. Cost estimates are ranges, not surveys, and I say so prominently. The free tier is 3 analyses, then it is a subscription, because every analysis costs me real inference money.Business model: subscriptions only. No commissions, no lead selling, no ads on analyses. The moment I earn from the transaction, I am not on the buyer's side anymore.I would love feedback on the analysis quality (there is a free tier, no signup needed) and on the harder question: how do you market a tool people need exactly once every ten years?
