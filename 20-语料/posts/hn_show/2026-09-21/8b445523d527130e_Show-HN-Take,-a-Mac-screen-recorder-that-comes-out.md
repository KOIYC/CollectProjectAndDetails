The staging
 Every capture lands finished.
Take records the screen and, separately, the truth about what happened on it: where the cursor
 went, when you clicked, which window moved. The staging is solved from that, which is why it can
 stay editable instead of being baked into pixels.
The backdrop
Padding, rounded corners and a true shadow, over a backdrop you pick. Set padding to zero and
 it fills the frame edge to edge. Save the look you like as the default and every future take
 arrives wearing it.
The cursor
Redrawn from its own track rather than filmed, so it stays crisp at 2.4x zoom. Spring-smoothed,
 resizable, with hide-when-idle and a spotlight that dims everything but the pointer.
The zooms
Solved from click clusters, then handed to you as clips on a timeline. Drag one to move it,
 tap the empty row to add one, delete the one that guessed wrong.
A library that is just a folder Takes are .mov files on disk. Delete one in
 Finder and it is gone, with nothing left behind to repair.
Inspectors that change the canvas Move padding and the preview moves under your
 finger. There is no Apply button because there is nothing to apply.
A timeline of real objects Zooms and idle skips sit on their own rows. The
 footer says exactly what the edit did to the running time.
The editing
 Objects, not renders.
Most recorders make you commit: apply the edit, wait for a render, watch it back,
 undo, wait again. Take runs one compositor for both the live canvas and the exported file, so
 the thing you are scrubbing IS the thing you will ship.
Preview equals export. Not approximately. The same code path draws both.
Blur what should not ship. Drag a box over a key or a customer name. It rides the
 timeline and burns into the file.
Keystrokes as keycaps. Shortcuts appear as you press them, so a terminal demo does
 not need narration to be followable.
Dead air speeds up, it does not vanish. Idle spans get a 4x ramp by default, and you
 can keep any of them at full speed.
Real render from the shipping compositor.

Captions, on device

Exports that tell the truth
MP4, ProRes, animated GIF and raw passthrough, each showing a real size estimate before you
 commit to it.

Scenes on hotkeys. Your screen, full screen, a be right back card, a title and an
 ending ship ready. Control command 1 through 9 switches with a smooth dissolve, or instantly
 with option held.
One click, mid-presentation. The bubble and the zoom have switches on the studio
 bar and on a floating bar that stays over any app. Hide your face or park the zoom without
 touching a menu while people watch.
Record what went out. A separate Record button saves a take of the exact frames the
 stream carried, on the same clock. It lands in your library and edits like any other take.

Questions
 Good to know.
Can I try Take before I buy?
 Yes. Take runs fully for 7 days with no card. The download needs no account: sign in inside the
 app to start the week. Nothing is watermarked during the trial or after it.
Does Take work offline?
 Yes. You need internet once to activate. Recording, staging, editing and exporting all run on
 your Mac using Core Image and Metal. Nothing you record is uploaded anywhere.
What format are the files, and is there a watermark?
 Takes are ordinary .mov files in an open folder, with a small sidecar next to each one holding
 the edits. There is no watermark, ever, and no proprietary project format to export out of.
Which Macs does Take run on?
 Any Mac on macOS 14 Sonoma or later. The app is universal, so Apple Silicon and Intel both run
 natively.
Can Take present live in Google Meet or Zoom?
 Yes. Open the Live Studio, go live, and pick the camera named Take Camera in Meet, Zoom,
 FaceTime or OBS. The call sees your staged screen: backdrop, camera bubble, zooms and scene
 switches, exactly what the studio monitor shows. Install the camera once from the studio and
 approve it once in System Settings.
How does Take compare to Screen Studio?
 Take matches it on automatic staging and adds live editing, where moving a zoom updates the
 canvas immediately because the preview and the export use the same compositor. Take also
 presents live through a virtual camera, which Screen Studio does not do. Screen Studio is
 subscription-only for new buyers and project-based; Take is one payment and plain files.
How do updates work?
 Take checks for updates by itself and can install them in place, and there is a Check for
 updates button in Settings if you would rather ask. Every update is free.
