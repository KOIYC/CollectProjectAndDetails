Questions
 Fair questions, straight answers.
 How is this different from Myers-Briggs or 16-type tests? + Type tests sort you into boxes; measurement doesn't support boxes. Personality traits are continuous and roughly normally distributed — most people are near the middle of most of them. The Big Five reports where you sit on each dimension instead of rounding you to the nearest archetype, which is why it's the framework used in essentially all modern personality research.
 How accurate will my results be? + As accurate as your answers. Self-report questionnaires with well-tested items predict real outcomes surprisingly well at the group level, but they are estimates, not X-rays: read your percentiles as bands, and expect scores to wobble a few points on a retake. If a result genuinely doesn't fit, trust the evidence of your life over the instrument.
 What happens to my data? + Your answers and scores are stored under an unguessable link that only you see, and nothing is shared or sold. An account is optional and needs only an email. You can read the details on the privacy page.
 What does the $2.99 buy, exactly? + The written interpretation layer: all thirty facets explained at your level, plus per-trait analyses for work, close relationships, and growth. It's a one-time purchase for that result — no subscription, and a 30-day refund if you don't find it accurate.
 Can I retake it? + Yes, free, as often as you like. Traits are fairly stable in adulthood, so expect broadly similar results — big swings usually mean a different frame of mind while answering, and that's information too.
 Is this a psychological evaluation? + No. Fiveprint measures normal-range personality for self-understanding. It is not a medical, clinical, or hiring instrument, and no facet score — including the ones with clinical-sounding names — is a diagnosis of anything.
