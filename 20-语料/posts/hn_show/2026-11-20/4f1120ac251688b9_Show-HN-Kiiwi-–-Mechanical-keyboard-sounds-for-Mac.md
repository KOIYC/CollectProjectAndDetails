Why this exists
I could hear
the shortcuts.
Kiiwi started as a fix for a sound that felt wrong. Here is what
 that took.
Most apps in this category are a folder of samples and a player. The
 first version of Kiiwi was too, and it sounded wrong in a way I could
 not stop noticing.
One pack had 60 milliseconds of near silence in front of every
 key. You don't hear that as silence, you hear it as lag. Every sample is
 now trimmed to 2 ms before the switch actually bottoms out, so the
 sound lands with your finger.
430 checks run before any build ships. Every switch resolves a
 sample for every key, every pack decodes, and the audio graph survives
 you unplugging your headphones. That is the part nobody notices, which
 is the whole point of doing it.

How do I get typing sounds on a Mac?
 Install a keyboard sound app that plays a mechanical keyboard sound for
 every key you press. Kiiwi is one: choose from 30+ real switch recordings, and
 it sounds in every app, on a MacBook's built-in keyboard or any external
 one, on macOS 14 or later. Hear every switch in the
 playground before you download, or read the
 guide to typing sounds for Mac .
 Weighing it against the others? See the
 best keyboard sound apps for Mac .
Does it work in every app?
 Yes. Kiiwi listens at the system level, so it sounds the same in your
 editor, your browser, Slack, Terminal and Spotlight. It never intercepts
 or alters your keystrokes. It only listens for them, so nothing you type
 is changed, delayed or swallowed.
Why does it need Accessibility permission?
 macOS requires it to observe key events system-wide. It's the same
 permission text-expanders and shortcut apps use. Kiiwi asks once, and the
 app detects the grant within about a second and a half, with no restart. If
 you'd rather not grant it, Kiiwi still runs but can only sound for keys
 typed in its own window.
Is anything I type recorded or sent anywhere?
 No. A key event is read only to decide which sample to play, then it's
 discarded. For your stats, Kiiwi adds one to a few counters: today, this
 hour, the keyboard row, the switch, and the app you're typing in. It
 never notes which key it was, and the counts stay in a file on your
 Mac. Kiiwi sends two things: your licence key, to check the licence is
 still valid (when you activate, on launch and once a day; offline it
 keeps working for 3 days), and a daily request for the update list.
 What you type never leaves your Mac because it is never held long
 enough to.
Can I see, export or delete my stats?
 Yes. Settings → Stats shows everything Kiiwi has counted. Export saves
 every day's counts as a JSON file, and Reset sets them back to zero (it
 keeps one copy beside the file, so a reset can be undone). Nothing about
 your stats ever leaves your Mac unless you post a share card
 yourself.
Will it slow my Mac down?
 No. Idle CPU is 0%, because nothing runs between keystrokes. A keypress costs
 one buffer schedule against already-decoded audio. Memory sits around
 58 MB because only the switch you're using is loaded.
