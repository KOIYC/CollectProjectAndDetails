A single-player strategy game of Rome’s rise. Govern provinces, raise legions and command

No download and no account. Your campaign saves itself in this browser.
What is Roman Times?
You lead Rome, or one of its rivals, from a single province to an empire. Each year you choose
 where to march, what to raise and how hard to tax; then the rival kingdoms take their turns. When
 armies meet, you fight the battle yourself.
A campaign across the Mediterranean
33 provinces from Britannia to Mesopotamia and 8 kingdoms. You win by
 holding 8, 12 or 16 provinces, as you chose
 before you began, and lose if your capital falls.
How a campaign works
Provinces to govern
Set each province’s tribute, hold games, station garrisons and appoint generals. Public
 mood, from 0 to 100, decides what a province pays and whether it rises against you.
How provinces work
Battles you command
Choose a formation and an opening tactic, then fight in real time: select cohorts, send them
 across open ground, draw the line they form, strike flanks and rears, and keep your army’s
 morale from breaking.
How battles work
How to start
Press Play
The title screen lets you choose the difficulty, the campaign’s length, its opening and the
 kingdom you lead. The first time, keep the defaults: Rome, standard length, normal difficulty.
Play your first year
Short tips walk you through it: claim a free province, raise troops in Italia, and attack a
 neighbour. Every claim, recruit and march can be taken back with Undo until you fight.
End the year
The rivals move, income arrives and public mood changes. The year’s report says what
 happened, and the next year is yours to plan.
What makes it interesting
Battles you fight, not watch. Cohorts fight on their own until you give them orders, which you give as the battle runs: to one cohort, a few, or the whole army. A blow to the rear does 60% more damage.
Every choice costs something. Heavy tribute fills the treasury and empties the province’s goodwill; an army that marches out of the capital leaves half of every troop type behind.
Rivals that play back. The other kingdoms recruit, appoint generals and govern too, and once Rome grows large its neighbours make pacts against it.
A career to climb. Battles, provinces and the Senate’s demands bring glory, from Tribune to Legate, Praetor and Consul. Each rank opens more: generals, alliances, marriages, client kingdoms.
Games and sea fights you play yourself. In your capital, drive a chariot round the Circus Maximus or fight a gladiator bout. At sea, steer a war galley to ram or board the enemy. These, and your battles, are drawn in 3D.
A different campaign each time. Two openings, three lengths, three difficulties, and any of 8 kingdoms to lead. Events such as plague, raids and good harvests ask you to choose.
Plays anywhere. Mouse, keyboard or touch, on a computer or a phone. After the first visit it opens without a connection and can be installed like an app.
In English or in Latin. The whole game, its title included, can be played in Latin.
