Self-care
 It takes care of itself.
A supervisor checks the whole device every 90 seconds. When a check confirms a fault, one of fifteen bounded repairs runs, is written down with the reason it was chosen, and, if it touches files, is verified and undone when it didn't work.
Every 90 seconds
Heals what gets stuck
It watches services, the model server, memory, disk, temperature, Wi-Fi and the screen. Fifteen repairs, each with a hard limit: never more than three restarts of one thing in half an hour.
7 of the 15
Fixes that undo themselves
Repairs that touch files or its search indexes run as a transaction: snapshot, fix, verify, then keep it or roll it back. Your own words are never rewritten from an index.
Cause, not symptom
Root cause first
It can tell a cause from a consequence. A repair aimed at a symptom is held back when the evidence points elsewhere, and when it can't tell, it says so.
Learns on your unit
Keeps score, and rehearses
It keeps score of every repair on your unit and prefers what has worked there. On quiet days it breaks something built to be broken and watches the real repair handle it.
Every week
A report in plain words
On the screen and on your phone: what went wrong this week, what it fixed, what it learned, and anything waiting for your OK.
Only with your OK
Improves, when you approve
When a better fix is handed to it, it builds and tests it in a sealed workspace that can't touch your data. It reaches the device only when you approve it and the update is signed, and it rolls back if the self-test fails.
