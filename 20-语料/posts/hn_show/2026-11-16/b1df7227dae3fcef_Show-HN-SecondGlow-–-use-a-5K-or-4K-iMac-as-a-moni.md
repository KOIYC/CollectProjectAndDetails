Using an iMac as an external display
 Common questions
 Can a 5K iMac be used as an external monitor?
 Not on its own. Apple’s Target Display Mode, which let older iMacs act as a monitor, never came to the 27-inch 5K iMac. With SecondGlow it can: your Mac sends its screen over one cable, and the iMac shows it at its full 5120 × 2880.
 Every way to use a 5K iMac as a monitor
 Is SecondGlow a Target Display Mode alternative?
 Yes. It does the job Target Display Mode did on older iMacs, in software, for the Retina iMacs from 2015 to 2020: the 27-inch 5K and the 21.5-inch 4K. The iMac stays on and runs SecondGlow too, free, and your Mac treats it as an external display.
 Does it work with the 21.5-inch iMac?
 Yes, with the 21.5-inch Retina 4K iMacs from 2015, 2017 and 2019, at their full 4096 × 2304. The 21.5-inch models with a 1920 × 1080 screen aren’t supported, because they don’t have a Retina screen.
 Does every 2015 iMac work?
 The late 2015 models do: the 27-inch 5K and the 21.5-inch Retina 4K, which run macOS Monterey. The 27-inch 5K from mid 2015 doesn’t, because like the late 2014 model its last macOS is Big Sur. To check, open Apple menu → About This Mac on the iMac: it names the model and year.
 Does it work with M1, M2, M3, M4 and M5 Macs?
 It’s made for any Mac with Apple silicon on macOS Ventura or later, M1 through M5. Macs with an Intel processor can’t send the picture.
 Do I need a dongle or adapter?
 No dongle and no adapter, just one cable. A USB-C to USB-A cable with USB 3 goes from your Mac to a USB-A port on the back of the iMac, and works on every supported iMac, including the 2015 models. On iMacs from 2017 on, a Thunderbolt or USB4 cable, USB-C on both ends, works too. Charging-only cables carry no data and won’t work. A USB 3 cable often has a blue tab inside the USB-A end, or an SS mark.
 How is it different from Luna Display?
 Luna Display needs its own hardware unit, $89.99 with the unit included, and works with many Macs and PCs, over Wi-Fi too. Astropad lists 5K at 45 Hz on a Mac. SecondGlow needs no extra hardware, just a USB cable, costs less, and is made only for Retina iMacs and Apple silicon Macs.
 SecondGlow vs Luna Display
 How is it different from RetinaRelay?
 RetinaRelay does the same job and works with Intel Macs and older iMacs, which SecondGlow doesn’t. SecondGlow costs less, covers up to 7 of your Macs with one license, encrypts the link between them, and sets the iMac’s brightness from your Mac.
 SecondGlow vs RetinaRelay
 Should I buy a new 5K display instead?
 If you want a camera and microphones, or one cable that also charges your laptop, a new display like Apple’s Studio Display is better, from $1,599. If you already have a 5K iMac and mostly want its screen, SecondGlow gives you that for $39. Try it free for 7 days before you decide.
 SecondGlow vs a new Studio Display
 Is there a free alternative?
 Yes. OpenDisplay, TargetBridge and Sharp are free and open source, and worth a try if you don’t mind some setup. OpenDisplay sends a video stream; its own measurements show about 30 fps at full 5K sharpness. SecondGlow sends a sharp picture with adaptive quality over a USB 3 cable instead, and I measure about 50 to 60 fps on full-screen 5K video.
 SecondGlow vs OpenDisplay
 How is it different from AirPlay?
 AirPlay to Mac is free and built into macOS, but works over Wi-Fi, and Apple only lists Macs from 2018 or later as receivers, so of the Retina iMacs only the 2019 and 2020 models and the iMac Pro can use it. SecondGlow works on every Retina iMac from 2015 to 2020, over a cable, at the iMac’s native resolution.
 Every way to use a 5K iMac as a monitor
 Does sound play through the iMac?
 Yes, on macOS Sonoma 14.2 or later. Turn on Sound on the iMac in SecondGlow on your Mac: your Mac’s sound plays through the iMac’s speakers in step with the picture, and your Mac’s volume and mute keys control it. Headphones and AirPods on your Mac still work as usual.
 Can I use the iMac’s keyboard or mouse?
 No. Like any display, the iMac shows your Mac’s screen, and keyboard and mouse stay with your Mac.
 Is the picture lossless?
 When the screen is still, yes: every pixel arrives exactly as your Mac drew it. When things move, adaptive quality compresses on the GPU to keep motion smooth, and goes back to lossless a couple of seconds after the screen settles.
 Does the iMac need to be on?
 Yes, with SecondGlow open on it. Turn on automatic login, and the iMac is ready as a display after every restart.
 Is it private?
 Yes. The picture travels only over the cable, encrypted, and never leaves your two Macs. No account. The apps send only a few anonymous setup counts, explained on the privacy page. Questions come straight to me at support@secondglow.app.
 Privacy
 My Mac says “Unlock to use accessories”. What do I do?
 That’s macOS checking a new USB connection, before SecondGlow is involved. Unlock your Mac, and click Allow if it asks. To plug in while your Mac is locked, open System Settings → Privacy & Security and set Allow accessories to connect to Always.
 What if it doesn’t work on my setup?
 Try it free for 7 days first, so you know before you pay. If something isn’t right, write to support@secondglow.app and I’ll help. If you’ve bought it, you can get a refund within 14 days.
