Pause AI for five years? - Fondue

## Goal for this session

A public Fondue demo for Hacker News readers, on one question: if you could pause AI development worldwide for five years, would you?

So far two people would probably pause and one probably wouldn't. Four arguments are open below, and yours could tip one or open a new one.

- In Fondue, everyone talks with their own AI at the same time, and another AI brings those conversations together on one board like this.
- Bluemo built Fondue for small team meetings and opened this board to try it with Hacker News readers.
- To join, talk through your answer with the AI for a few minutes. What you say lands under the argument it bears on, and each line links to the words it came from.
- Rashik, who works at a frontier AI lab, and Maya, a high school teacher, would probably pause. Bluemo probably wouldn't. Rashik's question to Bluemo, and to everyone who wouldn't pause, is still unanswered.

## 1. If we build AI much smarter than us, could we keep control of it?

Rashik would pause for this reason, and says it is the mainstream reason the board was missing. He honestly doesn't know whether we could keep control, and he doesn't think we should wait for proof that something will go wrong. The pull the other way is medical research: better AI might help develop treatments for diseases we can't cure today.

Still open: for those who wouldn't pause, is losing control unlikely, or are the benefits worth the risk?

## 3. If we don't pause, will we get locked into depending on AI?

Both answers so far share this worry: we are handing more and more over to AI, with little say in how it decides. The case for pausing is that once businesses and public services depend on it, deciding we don't like it won't be enough to undo it.

Still open: have you seen a team, a school or a service come to depend on a tool and then fail to back out?

- M What worries me is how hard it might become to change our minds later. Okay, so let me continue. Yeah, so, like, the part that worries me is that how hard it might become to change our minds later. So if we build our businesses and public services around increasingly capable AI, deciding we don't like the arrangement won't be enough. We may depend on it by then. So I don't know how quickly that happens. I also don't think everything about AI is bad. Keeping the models we already have that people keep finding useful things to do with them. So I'd take five years on that, even if it means waiting longer for better systems. So, yeah. Maya

- If we build our businesses and public services around increasingly capable AI, deciding we don't like the arrangement won't be enough, because we may depend on it by then. I don't know how quickly that happens.
- B I'm worried about people handing more decisions to AI without having much say in how those decisions get made. Also, another thing is that, like, I understand wanting five years to catch up, like, but, like, I'm worried about people handling more decisions to AI without having much say in how those decisions get made. But I'm not sure, like, that giving us more time would change that. Like, suppose we press this for development for five years. Like, what happens on the market? Like, who starts doing something they weren't? Schools still need resources. Governments still need people who understand the technology, and companies can't keep deploying the models that already exist. So I'd be, like, much more willing to press it if the post came with actual commitments for, like, funding for people responsible for the work and something we could check at the end. Like, five years is a long time to give up without knowing what we're getting. So I think what would change my mind is a convincing reason that this work can't happen while development continues. So if we are genuinely unable to adapt because the technology keeps changing, then a pause would make sense. But I haven't heard that with that question. Bluemo
