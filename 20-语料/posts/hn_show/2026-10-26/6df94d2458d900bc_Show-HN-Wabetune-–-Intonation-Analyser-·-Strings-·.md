 Your F♯ is in tune. Unless you are in D major.
Same recording, judged twice. Against equal temperament this F♯ is on the note.
 Declare D major and it sits fourteen cents above where the third of the chord
 belongs: a pure third is 13.7 cents under the piano's.
That is the number a quartet tunes to. A tuner will say the note is fine. Your
 cellist will say it beats.
The top D stays sharp in both. No key helps with that one.
Just intonation, and the twenty-two cents most apps ignore.
An F♯ above a D has three defensible places to be.
Equal 400.0
Just 5:4 386.3

 Tune the chord, not just the note.

The.whole.of.it
 Everything the instrument does.
Written out, so you know what it measures.
01 · Instruments
 Whose intonation it measures
Strings: violin, viola, cello, double bass (five-string and C-extension too), fretless bass, oud: wherever a finger places the note, not a fret.
Woodwind: oboe, clarinet, flutes, recorders, saxophones, bassoons.
Brass: horn, trumpets, trombone and low brass.
Voice: every register, soprano to bass.
The double bass reads below 42 Hz, where most pitch detection fails.
Not the piano, marimba or organ: there the pitch is decided before you play.
02 · Listening
 What it hears
All the notes your instrument can sound, on one map.
A note's pitch is the median of its readings, the bowed attack discarded.
A cell averages what landed on it, weighted by how long you held it , over a window you choose.
Off by (sharp or flat) and swing (consistency between attempts) stay separate.
When it cannot confirm a note's octave, it marks the note and keeps it.
03 · Declaring
 What you tell it
The tonic and the tuning system, and nothing else.
Counted from a tonic , so it follows the key: just intonation and Pythagorean.
Counted from the keyboard , fixed in every key: equal, quarter- and sixth-comma meantone, Vallotti, Werckmeister III, Kirnberger III. Equal is the default.
A degree no system places stays blank, drawn differently from zero.
04 · Spectrum
 What it hears together
Everyone in the room at once, and the chord they make, by name.
Each note against the tuning system you agreed on, as on the map.
And against the pure interval over the chord's root, as it was played.
Not a session: nothing is recorded. Two minutes a day on the free plan.
05 · Setting
 What you can move
Five levels of in tune , from ten cents down to two.
Concert A from 390 to 460 , 442 by default. Changing it starts a new session.
Transposition, for an instrument written in one key and sounding in another.
06 · Reporting
 What you get back
The note furthest out and the note least steady, with the figures.
Note by note: deviation, swing and times played, over the whole session .
Intervals that keep coming back wrong, in the same direction: a third that is always too narrow.
It flags notes with an unconfirmed octave.
Hear it back: the session replays against its own map.
Share a window (a week, a month, a year) as a PDF for your teacher.
