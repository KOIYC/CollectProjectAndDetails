# Damon Chen: Testimonial.to from $1.99 in Failures to $100K+ MRR

Cisco engineer who quit after a family promise, reused failed-project code, and bootstrapped a video testimonial SaaS via Product Hunt, build-in-public, affiliates…

Damon Chen spent eight years as a software engineer at Cisco. When COVID hit in 2020 he was working from home with his wife and one-year-old. He took six months of unpaid leave to try building products. The first four side projects (Indielog, Howdy, Influeswer, and Backlogs.co) generated a combined $1.99.

Testimonial.to was the fifth. It solved a problem he knew firsthand: collecting authentic social proof for his own failed products. Video and text testimonials felt more credible than generic one-liners, yet most tools made the process clunky. He reused roughly 80% of the code from earlier work, built an MVP in about a week (or less, depending on the account), added a landing page and Stripe, and launched on Product Hunt in December 2020.

Early sales came from lifetime deals at $199. Public posts and Indie Hackers updates show roughly $3,000 in the first 10 days and around $6,000 in the first two weeks from about 30 deals. That was the first real revenue after months of near-zero. He quickly shifted away from one-time licenses toward subscriptions so the business could compound.

...

By September 2021—nine months after the launch and well inside the one-year window—he publicly reported hitting $100K ARR. The promise had been met. Later self-reported milestones included higher six-figure and seven-figure ARR figures for Testimonial.to itself.

...

Early customers came from the Indie Hackers community, Twitter/X, and the Product Hunt launch. Chen documented the journey publicly: milestones, pricing experiments, feature decisions, and the reality of juggling a young family. He has described build-in-public as a flywheel—shipping features that excite existing users while exposing the product to new ones.

Two structural growth loops mattered more than pure audience size (he has noted starting with under 1,000 Twitter followers). First, the “Wall of Love”: customers embed testimonials on their sites with a Testimonial.to logo. Visitors who like what they see sign up. The logo also created an upgrade incentive to remove it. Second, an affiliate program offering 30% recurring commission (and a first-year discount for the referred customer). Chen has said this channel contributed roughly 10–15% of revenue. He mentioned the program in onboarding emails so new users could opt in.

...

In November 2025 he posted that Testimonial.to was at $109K MRR, describing it as “just a traditional app. No AI, no hype. Not a hockey-stick curve, but $109K MRR still feels good.” Earlier public figures (for example, around $70–83K MRR in 2023–2024) show steady compounding rather than viral spikes. Third-party sites have published higher aggregate portfolio estimates; those are not treated here as primary self-reported numbers for the single product.

He stayed largely solo until around $400K ARR, at which point he made a first hire focused on marketing. The company has remained lean. In late 2026 he publicly noted replacing Intercom on Testimonial with his own product, chat.sh.
