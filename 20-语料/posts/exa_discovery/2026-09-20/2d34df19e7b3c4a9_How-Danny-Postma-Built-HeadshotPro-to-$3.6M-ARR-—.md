In June 2020, during the first pandemic lockdown, Danny Postma took an eBook he’d written about writing better headlines — a $19 download — and turned it into software. He was a self-taught developer living in Bali, Indonesia, with no funding, no co-founder, and no plan beyond shipping something people would pay for.

The software was Headlime: an AI tool that generated marketing copy. Eight months later, it belonged to someone else, for a seven-figure sum. That wasn’t the end of the story. It was the first data point in a pattern Postma has now repeated twice.

Headlime found its market fast. By February 2021, it was generating $20,000 in monthly recurring revenue — a single founder, working alone, competing in a category that would soon be flooded with venture-backed AI copywriting startups.

HeadshotPro let users upload a handful of selfies and get back dozens of studio-quality professional headshots — generated by AI, styled with different backdrops and outfits, priced for the LinkedIn-photo problem millions of remote and hybrid workers suddenly had. Postma built it, by multiple accounts, in about a month.
...
The launch went further and faster than Headlime had. According to reporting on the launch by Starter Story and GreyJournal, HeadshotPro did six figures in sales within its first weeks online, driven by the same channel Postma had used before: building in public, largely on X, rather than paid acquisition.
...
The growth didn’t taper. Independent business trackers — GetLatka’s revenue database and an Indie Hackers breakdown of the product’s SEO strategy — have both put HeadshotPro at approximately $300,000 in monthly recurring revenue, roughly $3.6 million a year, achieved within about a year of launch. A case study published by Rewardful, the affiliate-tracking platform HeadshotPro uses, credits its affiliate program alone with contributing more than $50,000 of that in a typical month.
...
For a stretch, HeadshotPro was, by Postma’s own account and the accounts of people who’ve tracked his business, a company with a headcount of one. He wrote the product, handled the marketing, ran the SEO strategy that put HeadshotPro at the top of search results for “professional headshot” queries, and answered support himself. As revenue scaled past six figures a month, he began bringing on a small team — the pattern nearly every successful solo-built product eventually follows, because the tools that let one person build a product don’t scale the same way to running a support queue and a growing affiliate network.
...
What didn’t change was the funding structure. No venture round. No outside capital. Two products, two real outcomes — a seven-figure acquisition and a business generating millions a year — both financed entirely by the revenue the products themselves produced.
...
Danny Postma is a Dutch, self-taught developer based in Bali, Indonesia. He founded Headlime, an AI copywriting tool acquired by Conversion.ai in 2021, and later HeadshotPro, an AI headshot generator that reached roughly $300,000 in monthly recurring revenue as a solo-built product.
