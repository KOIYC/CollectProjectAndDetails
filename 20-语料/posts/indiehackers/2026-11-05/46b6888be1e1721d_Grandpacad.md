 Public LLM Bencmarks are BS. You need your own
I've been following a bunch of different benchmarks to help me decide which LLM model should I use for GrandpaCAD. It turns out models are very jagged - meaning they excel at certain task (refactoring a code base for example), and suck at others (should I walk or take a car to a car wash).
 I wrote more about it here: https://grandpacad.com/en/blog/public-benchmarks-misled-me-opus-4-7
 The point is, build your benchmarks/evals as soon as possible, otherwise you may be under performing or overpaying or both for same results.

 The Ultimate Niche: I Built an AI CAD Tool So My Grandpa Could Design Again
We always hear about solving problems you care about. For me, that meant solving my Grandpa's problem.
 My grandfather, a lifelong maker, lost his mobility and couldn't access his workshop. We got him a 3D printer, but traditional CAD software (Fusion 360, etc.) was too complex, too many functions, tough mouse control, and a language barrier (he speaks Slovenian). He was stuck just printing other people's designs.
 The Indie Hackers Lesson: The problem wasn't the printer; it was the interface.
 The "Aha" Moment: He loved using ChatGPT for simple questions. That’s when the lightbulb went off: What if creating a 3D model was as simple as describing it conversationally?
 I started building GrandpaCAD . We use LLMs to translate simple, natural language ("Make me a small box with a lid") directly into geometric code.
 The Takeaway:
 Niche Down to One User: We focused only on simple, functional parts. Exactly what he needed. Don't try to beat Fusion 360; focus where a simple conversational interface shines brightest.
Solve a Deeply Personal Pain: Seeing him successfully design and print his own creation for the first time was the true "validation." The frustration vanished, replaced by creative joy.
Features For Him: He didn't grew up using computer like we did, so icons don't mean much to him. Small buttons are hard to click,... So there is a specific feature for him "Grandpa Mode" that makes interface even simpler (for the cost of aesthetics)
If you’re struggling to find your product-market fit, try solving the most specific, personal problem you can find. It might just lead to a product that helps more people than you ever imagined.
