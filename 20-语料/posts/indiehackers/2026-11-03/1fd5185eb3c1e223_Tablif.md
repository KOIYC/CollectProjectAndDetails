November 1, 2026
 Tablif started as a stock market for launches. Then my payment provider said no.
Part 1 of 5: how Tablif became a town.
 Tablif started with a simple idea. A launch disappears after one day. What if launches worked like a stock market? People could make virtual investments in new products, and a product would stay visible for longer than its launch day.
 So we started collecting every new launch, every day, and built the system around it.
 After a while I noticed something. A launch is a one-day flame. It burns bright and it's gone. And with the stock market idea, we weren't really doing anything different from the launch sites themselves. We were just another board.
 So I changed direction. We had all these launches and their websites, so we used AI to read them and turned Tablif into a tool for competitor analysis and finding leads. Every day it showed you the likely customers and the rivals of your project.
 The results were interesting. I checked them many times, and a lot of them were genuinely useful.
 But then I noticed something else. Most people who signed up didn't come for rivals or customers. They wanted their project added to the launch lists we were collecting from. And once they were on the list, they left. Very few people came for the analysis itself.
 I didn't change direction. I kept going.
 Until our payment provider rejected the project. Because of how it was built, on top of lead and marketing data, they wouldn't approve it for payments.
 That was the day Tablif Town was born.
