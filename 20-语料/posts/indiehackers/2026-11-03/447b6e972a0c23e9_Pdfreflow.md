 A one-minute explainer film, and what moved installs
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vtVpbWEYFDc
 I made a one-minute explainer film: a two-column paper on a phone, the pinch-and-scroll dance, then the same paper reflowed into one column.
 Since my last update the layout engine went through 45 versions, all now with testers: charts drawn as vector art arrive as pictures, drop caps no longer split a paragraph, line-break hyphens rejoin, and a book's index is no longer read as footnotes.
 Distribution: the dev communities brought no measurable installs. r/FlutterDev, for example, got 2.6K views, nearly all in the first six hours, and no comments.
 The best result so far was r/ebooks, a post aimed at readers instead of builders: "Some books only come as PDF - I built a reader that reflows them on a phone". It was #1 on the sub that day with 62 upvotes, brought 134 site visits by that evening, and was still drawing views two days later. It is at 31K views now. A second reader post, on r/Onyx_Boox, is at 7K.
 My read on why: the topic fit, because people on r/ebooks already have the problem the app solves. And the sub is quiet: few new posts a day keep a post on top for hours, so 62 upvotes were enough to hold #1.

 PDFreflow is in public beta - here's what the first readers say
PDFreflow turns PDFs made for paper - two-column papers, textbooks, manuals - into one clean column on your phone. No pinch-zooming, no sideways scrolling, and a real night mode for reading in bed without a lamp.
 It's now in open beta on Android and iOS, free while in beta.
 What the first readers wrote (Google Play beta feedback):
 - "Reflow is perfect, everything seems original and easy to read. The night mode is second to none." - Gabriel
 - "No ads, no forced synch, no forced login, no AI, no options that nobody wants. Just displays and reflows." - Aleksandra
 - "Exactly what I was looking for ... a reflow effect that almost no other app has." - María (translated from Spanish)
 - "Very good, it looks like a Kindle, I really liked it." - Carlos (translated from Portuguese)
 The model: short PDFs (most papers) stay free after launch. Long documents will need a single unlock - one purchase, all documents, any length, forever. No subscription, no ads.
 What helps most right now: if a PDF reads badly, long-press the text, tap 'Feedback…' and tick 'Upload the current document'. I read every single report and reply in the app.
