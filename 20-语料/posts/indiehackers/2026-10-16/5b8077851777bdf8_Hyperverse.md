October 15, 2026
 I missed nothing in the LLM wave. I’m not missing the robot one.
Two years ago I shipped FakeChatGPT.com . It’s a silly little tool that makes ChatGPT conversations that look real. People used it for memes, pranks and marketing mockups. It got [X] users and [X] visits a month, and it paid for itself from day one.
 It also taught me the most important lesson of my career:
 The money follows the wave, and the best time to get on it is before everyone sees it.
 I was early to the LLM wave only because I was playing with it. Fake chats were a joke, but behind the joke I was watching a technology go from “neat demo” to thousends of users in about 18 months.
 So I started asking a different question: what’s the next ChatGPT moment, and where are the shovels?
 My answer: robots.
 The moment no one’s talking about (yet)
 Physical AI is where LLMs were in 2021. Foundation models for robots are improving every month. Humanoids are walking out of labs and into warehouses and supermarkets. Billions are flowing in.
 But the industry has a dirty secret: nobody can test their robots properly.
 When you train a new robot policy, how do you know it’s better than the last one? Today the answer is usually “we ran it on the real robot a few times and it looked okay.” Picture shipping software with no tests, where every test costs an hour of hardware time and might break a $100k arm.
 LLMs got benchmarks, evals and leaderboards. Robots got vibes.
 So I built Hyperverse
 Hyperverse.sh is CI/CD for robot brains.
 📸 Real places become test arenas. We scan real spaces (a supermarket, an office, a family home) into photorealistic 3D worlds.
🤖 Every checkpoint gets scored. Point Hyperverse at a policy and it runs your benchmark suites in those arenas, then tells you if you got better or worse.
🔁 It runs on every training run, automatically. Like GitHub Actions, but for robots.
The jump from a meme generator to physics simulation and gaussian splats sounds huge. It wasn’t. The playbook was the same:
 Find the wave early
Build the boring, necessary tool everyone will need
Ship ugly, ship fast, talk to users
The tech changed. The instinct didn’t.
 Why I’m telling you this now
 Every gold rush has a window. For LLM wrappers it closed fast. In 2023 you could launch a GPT tool on a weekend and make $10k a month. By 2024 the space was packed.
 Physical AI is in that 2022 window right now. The tools aren’t built yet. The incumbents don’t exist yet. The indie hackers who move first will own categories.
