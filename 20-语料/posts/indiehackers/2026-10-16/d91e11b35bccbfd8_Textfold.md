October 15, 2026
 Day 2: I shipped what the comments on my launch post asked for
Yesterday I launched Textfold, a text expander for Mac, and asked for feedback on pricing and switching. The comments were better than I expected, so today's update (1.0.4) is built almost entirely from them.
 1. Importing is now free.
 Someone pointed out that people only switch if their existing snippets come over in one click. Textfold could already import from TextExpander, Espanso, aText, Apple text replacements and CSV, but I'd made it a Pro feature. That was backwards: if you can't try it with your own snippets, you never get to the point of buying. Now anyone can import everything.
 2. A clear screen at snippet 11.
 Another comment said the moment that sells Pro is when someone saves snippet 11, so that screen deserves as much care as the landing page. Mine showed a small orange note, and worse, a new snippet could quietly switch off an old one. Now saving or importing past the free limit says plainly what stays free, what one payment unlocks, and when the launch price ends, with a real date (November 8) instead of "during launch". Your oldest snippets always stay active.
 3. Counting it, without tracking anyone.
 The same comment suggested measuring how many people hit the cap. I promised no analytics inside the app, so I didn't add any. Instead, the Upgrade button on that screen opens a tagged page on my website, so I can count how many people reach it without the app sending anything.
 4. The landing page demo.
 One commenter only saw the finished email, not the moment ;sig turns into a signature. Turns out visitors with Reduce Motion turned on got a static version. Now it plays once for them too.
 Thanks to everyone who took the time to write. Next up: Product Hunt in about ten days, and talking to a few people who use TextExpander, Raycast or Espanso about what would make them switch.
 What should I look at next?

October 15, 2026
 Day 1: Textfold is live, and the first feedback already changed my plan
Textfold is live. It's a text expander for Mac: type a short abbreviation like ;sig and it turns into your signature, a reply or a whole email with blanks to fill in, in any app.
Where I posted today: Hacker News, X, Indie Hackers and a Reddit comment thread.
 The best feedback so far came from a comment on my launch post. Someone pointed out that people only switch text expanders if their existing snippets come over in one click. Textfold can already import from TextExpander, Espanso, Apple text replacements, aText and CSV, but I'd made importing a Pro feature. That's backwards: if you can't bring your snippets over to try it, you never get to the point of buying.
 So that's my next change: importing will be free, so anyone can see all their snippets in Textfold before deciding.
 Full story of why I built it and the decisions behind it: https://www.indiehackers.com/post/i-launched-my-first-mac-app-today-a-text-expander-with-plain-text-snippets-7ee42de7dd?commentId=-P4-17QLtbP6p0lhDdGi
