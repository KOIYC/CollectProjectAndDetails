November 5, 2026
 Rebranded the service and made some other changes
Today, I've finished the rebranding my service!
 Before, it was called KiteDocs and now KiteContent.
 Why?
 Because I don't want to focus solely on the documentation part of the content that potentially any product may have. And it's not only about finding "more users", it's rather about servicing my own needs.
 As many of you, I have tons of ideas. And I want to implement some of them. And while building a product is much easier right now (you know why :) the marketing part is still a challenge, for anybody.
 Being an introvert, it's super hard for me to send cold emails, chase users, and try to convince anybody to use my product (once, I tried - spent 2 f***g hours for writing one email, then 20 minutes to send it (yes, 20 minutes to click a f**g "Send" button), then I swore I won't never do it again.
 So, I decide I will not do it. But what else can I do?
 Yes, the content marketing is one (not only one) of methods to get a traction without chasing every single potential user. Some say that in our AI era SEO is not relevant anymore, but I read many articles and even books, and saw many use cases to claim "It's only only working these crazy AI days, but it gives more new opportunities for content marketing." Although, I can't skip the fact that nowadays it becomes a bit more sophisticated.
 Anyway, for the first steps, SEO and content marketing are perfect tools to tell the world about your great product.
 And this is how KiteContent helps.
 Not only you provide your users with great source of the information they can rely on and review if they have any questions, but it becomes a great marketing tool, and, if done right (and KiteContent does it right) can bring you real customers.
 But apparently, just documentation is not enough. You also want to write some blog articles, publish researches, do comparison pages, create and publish pSEO (programmatic SEO) pages, and so on. And in this case, you don't want to keep a strict structure what you did when writing your documentation but rather organize all articles using categories and tags.
 This part is not implemented yet. But I have a clear vision and hopefully, soon, I will have the comprehensive tool to cover all the basic SEO/content marketing needs for any product (okay, for almost any product) - this is exactly why the product must be called KiteContent, not KiteDocs.
 In the following post, I will discuss, how come that in our AI era, vibecoding documentation and content for your website is still very thin ice that (from my observations) is easily and often broken.

 Documentation must not be that hard
Like many of you, I start multiple projects. Every new idea, after a short validation (sometimes without any), transforms into an application, and every application needs a website, and every website needs some way to be promoted.
 One of very effective way to be noticed (and to look professionally and trustworthy) is to provide an app documentation. Like this:
 It must not be complicated, it may consist only of several pages explaining how to work with an app. Even though it could seem unnecessarily for small project, for me, as an end-user, it's a crucial thing: before buying, not, before starting a trial, no, even before giving someone my email, I always check their documentation, and leave immediately if found nothing.
 So, as a developer, I always tried to make things myself. But the documentation is not that easy to make: not only it's still challenge to create a nice UI and UX, but adding controls to create and update content and change such things as palette or icons, are still not easy, even with AI.
 I tried several open-source solutions and even used one in production, but it was always a challenge to update it.
 The installation is another story: I see that the absolute most of founders install their documentation to the subdomain, not a subfolder which look much more professionally and brings benefits from SEO point of view.
 Why is that? Because it's crazy hard. I figured it out for Linux and provide this for my documentation, see what you should do to set up the documentation under your custom folder:
 But I guess not everyone is using Linux and may have direct access to it.
 I want to provide as many ways to integrate the great documentation into your website as possible.
