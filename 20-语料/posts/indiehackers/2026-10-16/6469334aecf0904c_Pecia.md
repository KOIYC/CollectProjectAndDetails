 Why I built a reading library that watches second-hand book prices
So I built Pecia. You keep what you've read and what you want to read, on paper or on your Kindle or Kobo. Pecia suggests what comes next and watches the price. It's mainly for second-hand books, in any edition and with shipping included, but it also covers new books and ebooks. For now that means bookshops in Spain.
 A few decisions I made early:
 Mobile-first. Most reading decisions happen on a phone, in a queue or on the sofa, so Pecia is designed for the phone first.
 The library comes first, the price second. The point isn't saving a few euros. It's knowing what you want to read next.
 Try it without an account. Look up a book's price right away, with no sign-up.
 Free reading tools. A shareable nine-books grid, a Goodreads list import, and EPUB/PDF converters and validators.
 No ads, no selling your data.
 The name comes from the pecia system that 13th-century universities used to rent out books by the quire, so people who couldn't buy them could copy them. We didn't invent reusing books.
 It's in open beta now. I'd love to hear from other readers: how do you decide what to read next, and do you ever check second-hand first?
