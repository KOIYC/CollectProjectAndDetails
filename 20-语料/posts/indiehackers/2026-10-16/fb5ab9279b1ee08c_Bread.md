October 13, 2026
 The coding agent era broke open-source economics for solo developers
If you build developer software in public, you have probably felt this exact squeeze.
 You want people to inspect your code, build cool things with it, and talk about it. You want the distribution and feedback that only building in public can give you. So you slap an MIT or Apache license on your repo.
Then reality hits.
We are living in the coding agent era now. People do not just file bug reports anymore. Instead, they unleash an AI agent on your repo and overwhelm you with unsolicited, half baked 800 line pull requests that they expect you to review and maintain.
 Meanwhile, profitable companies quietly bake your code into their automated engineering pipelines and internal tools. They capture all the value while you triage PR notifications over dinner for a single $5 monthly GitHub Sponsorship.
 The standard advice on tech X is always the same: "Dual license it under AGPL and put up a 'Contact Sales' button."
Except, as an indie hacker, I do not want to run an enterprise sales department. I do not want to spend my week sitting on procurement Zoom calls, redlining vendor contracts, or chasing accounts payable teams for wire transfers.
I needed a third option: an honest way to build in public and get paid by profitable companies with zero sales calls.
How are you handling enterprise usage on your public projects today? Have you given up on open source entirely?
