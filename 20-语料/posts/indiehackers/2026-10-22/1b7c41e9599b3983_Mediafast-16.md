 What 6 Reddit bans taught me about marketing on Reddit (and the tool I built from it)
10 months, 6 suspensions, 1 post with 510K views. Everything I learned about marketing on Reddit without getting banned
 A while back I decided Reddit would be my main growth channel. Free traffic, millions of people talking about exactly the problems founders solve, no ad budget needed. Seemed obvious.
 Over the next 10 months I got suspended 6 times.
 Not warned. Suspended. [one line on the worst one, e.g. which sub, what you posted, how fast it happened]
 Eventually it clicked. I grew to 450+ karma and one of my posts hit 510K views. Here's what I wish someone had told me on day one.
 1. Your account is on trial before anyone reads your post
 A brand new account posting links looks exactly like a spam bot, to both Reddit's filters and the mods. Most of my early posts never had a chance. They were removed before a single human saw them.
 The fix is boring: comment for a few weeks first. Be useful in threads that have nothing to do with your product. Karma and account age are your ticket in.
 2. Every subreddit is its own country with its own laws
 r/SaaS, r/Entrepreneur and r/startups all look similar, but their rules on self-promo are completely different. Some ban links outright. Some allow them only on certain days. Some want a specific flair. Some quietly auto-remove anything with your domain in it.
 I got burned by rules I never read. Now I read the sidebar and the pinned posts before I post anywhere, every time.
 3. Shadowbans are the silent killer
 The worst part of one of my bans: I didn't know about it. I kept posting for days into nothing. No errors, no notification. Nobody could see my posts.
 If your posts suddenly get zero engagement, check your account in a logged out browser before you post again. I also made a free checker for this:
 Reddit Shadowban Detector
 4. Nobody on Reddit cares about your product. They care about their problem
 My first posts were basically "I built X, check it out." Dead on arrival.
 The post that hit 510K views didn't lead with my product at all. [one line on what it was about] It told a story and gave away something useful, and people found me from there.
 5. Comments beat posts
 Posts get the attention, but comments get the customers. Someone asks "what tool do you use for X?" and you give a real, honest answer. Those threads keep sending traffic for months because they rank on Google too.
 6. Consistency beats one viral hit
 The viral post was great. But the traffic that stuck came from showing up in the same few communities week after week until people recognized my name.
 So I built the thing I needed
 After 10 months of learning this the painful way, I turned it into a tool:
 MediaFast
 You tell it what you built, and every day it gives you a plan: which subreddits fit your product, which threads to comment on right now, and posts written to match each sub's rules so you don't get removed or banned. You still do the posting. It just does the research and guesswork that took me 10 months.
 150+ founders are using it so far, and now I'm sharing it with Indie Hackers.

It is called MediaFast ( https://www.mediafa.st ). It also tracks your karma and tells you if an account got shadowbanned before you waste a week posting into the void. Still just me building it, no cofounder, no employees, no ads.
Why I never did a proper launch until now: I was scared of the exact thing I built the tool to fix. Promoting on Reddit as a Reddit tool felt like a trap. X: 13,100 / 97
Direct: 12,200 / 112
Google: 5,200 / 38

Reddit: 480 / 0
Perplexity: 69 / 2
Product Hunt, BetaList, Uneed, TinyLaunch and about 10 other directories combined: about 700 / 2
Total: 36K visitors, 334 payments, $49.6K, zero paid ads.
That Reddit line is the funniest thing in the table. A Reddit marketing tool with 480 Reddit visitors and $0. The Reddit app drops the referrer when people click out, so almost all of it lands in Direct, which is my best converting channel. Most of my customers saw the name in a comment and typed it in. Analytics will tell you Reddit is dead while it quietly pays your rent.

X is the biggest number and the weakest one, under 1% conversion. IH converts better than X. Launch directories were a wasted Saturday.

Stop posting about your product. Answer threads where people already have the problem, and mention what you built only when it is the honest answer. That switch is where most of the 400 came from.
