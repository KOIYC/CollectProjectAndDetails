October 6, 2026
 I launched a $9 one-time Mac image compressor today — here's why I refused to make it a subscription
Six months ago I kept hitting the same wall: I write a blog, and every post means compressing screenshots, resizing them, and uploading them somewhere. Online compressors work, but they require uploading my files to someone else's server — not great for work screenshots. Local tools are private but single-purpose: compress, and then I still had to resize, convert and upload by hand. So I built TinyOwl — a local image compressor for macOS that does the whole loop in one place:
 - 100% local compression (PNG, JPG, JPEG, WebP, BMP, TIFF, AVIF) — nothing leaves your Mac unless a workflow says so
 - Reusable presets: resize → compress → convert → upload, run from the menu bar, clipboard or Finder
 - Optional direct upload to Cloudflare R2, S3, Alibaba OSS, Tencent COS or MinIO — with a public URL for every file
 - Rust-powered, fast on Apple Silicon I deliberately priced it as a one-time $9 (regular $15, 7-day free trial). No subscription, no account, no telemetry.
 I'd love your take on two specific things:
 1. Does a one-time $9 price actually make you *more* suspicious about long-term support? Or is "no subscription" a feature?
 2. The upload step (R2/S3/OSS/COS) — is that a workflow you'd use, or is pure local compression enough? I'm trying to decide where to focus next. Happy to answer questions about the compression engine or anything else. → https://tinyowl.app/
