 Websites Imagined Voice First
Every interface we use was designed around what computers could understand, not how people naturally communicate. So we learned to click, scroll, fill in forms, and write bios about ourselves in the third person. It worked. It was never natural.
 That's ending. Software can now listen and talk back like a person — in your voice, not a generic one — which makes voice the next interface the way touch was the last. Steer is built on that conviction, and it starts where the stakes are personal: the page about you.
 Setup is a conversation. Steer asks you a few questions, you answer out loud for about a minute, and from that recording it writes your page in your own phrasing and clones your voice. Put the link in your bio and you're done. A recruiter at 2 a.m., a client asking your rates, your kids asking what's for dinner — each of them hears you answer, instantly. The site itself works the same way: steer.social opens talking, because a voice-first company should.
 It is built to be trusted with your voice: a page answers only from what you wrote, strangers can't make it say anything you didn't, and pages can be locked with a code for family.
 Steer is the gateway. Personal pages first; then the web apps people work in, steered by talking to them; then the apps on their phones; then the things in their homes that never needed a screen. Voice-first means talking is the default and the screen is the fallback. Steer is the first product that takes that seriously for ordinary people — free to start, five minutes from "I should update my bio" to a page that speaks for you.
