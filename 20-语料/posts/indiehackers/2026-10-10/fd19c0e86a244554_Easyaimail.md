 Why I built EasyAImail after years of writing software
I have spent much of my working life building software. I am comfortable debugging code, designing systems, and working through technical problems.
 Writing an important email can still take me longer than I expect.
 The difficulty is rarely grammar alone. Before sending, I may need to decide what to include, what tone fits the relationship, whether I have missed an important detail, and how to make the result feel more considered than a plain block of text.
 This becomes harder when the email matters personally: thanking someone, apologizing, contacting a former professor, following up after an interview, writing to a new client, or inviting people to an event.
 You know why you want to write, but you may not know how to begin.
 The gap I kept seeing
 General AI assistants are useful for drafting text, but the user often has to provide a large prompt containing every relevant detail.
 If something is missing, the first draft may sound polished while still being incomplete.
 Traditional email builders solve a different part of the problem. They provide layouts and design controls, but the user still has to choose a suitable template, write the content, organize it, and make sure the result works as an email.
 I wanted to connect those steps.
 That became EasyAImail, a conversational tool for creating human-to-human emails rather than bulk marketing campaigns.
 Starting with a simple request
 A user should not need to prepare a perfect prompt.
 They can begin with something as simple as:
 “I want to thank a former professor.”
 “I need to tell a client that a project will be delayed.”
 “I want to invite some friends to a Halloween party.”
 Once the intention is clear, EasyAImail can recommend templates that fit the situation. The user can open the template details, compare the design and example, and decide which one to use.
 This distinction matters to me: the AI recommends a template, but it does not silently choose one for the user.
 People have different tastes, roles, relationships, and expectations. A design that feels appropriate to one sender may feel too formal or too playful to another. The decision should remain with the person writing the email.
 After that, the conversation continues. The AI asks for missing information such as the recipient relationship, important facts, preferred tone, dates, links, or other details the email may need.
 Only when the necessary information has been collected does it prepare the email.
 Why the result is an HTML email
 I did not want the workflow to end with a paragraph that still had to be copied into another tool and designed from scratch.
 The result is a complete HTML email using the template selected by the user. The sender can review both the content and the visual presentation before deciding whether it is ready.
 Email HTML is also less straightforward than normal web development. Different email clients do not render the same code consistently, so the generated result needs a reliable structure and appropriate styling rather than simply looking correct in a browser.
 There is still an important boundary: generating a polished email does not remove the sender’s responsibility.
 Names, dates, addresses, facts, tone, and relationship details must be checked before sending. AI can reduce some common writing and presentation work, but the user remains responsible for the final message.
 Building for three languages
 EasyAImail currently has English, Simplified Chinese, and Spanish interfaces.
 Each language has its own template library. I chose this approach because multilingual email design is not just a matter of translating interface strings. The appropriate wording, examples, visual preferences, and available templates can differ by language.
 The language selected in the interface determines the language used for the email conversation and generation. This keeps each workflow consistent instead of mixing languages unexpectedly.
 Supporting three languages has added work to almost every part of the product, from templates and prompts to documentation and content. It has also made me think more carefully about what should be shared across languages and what should be localized.
 What I am learning now
 The product is live, but I am still at an early stage.
 My background is in technical development, not marketing. Much of my current work is learning how to find the people who genuinely experience this problem.
 I have started reading real questions in communities where people ask for help with difficult emails. Those questions are useful not only for promotion, but for understanding what the product and template library are missing.
 Sometimes the answer is better writing guidance. Sometimes a suitable template already exists. Sometimes the honest conclusion is that the template library does not yet cover the situation well enough.
 That research is beginning to influence what I build next.
