 A free, no-sign-up tool to change existing logo colors
Your logo might look great in color, but what about invoices, dark website headers, or one-color printing? Sometimes you just need a different version of the same logo, not a redesign.
 We built a small tool to make that easier. You can upload an existing logo, change individual colors, or turn the whole logo black or white in one click.
 A few things we wanted to keep simple:
 Completely free, with no sign-up required
Colors are detected automatically, so you can change them directly
Transparent backgrounds are preserved when downloading
Everything runs in your browser, so your logo never leaves your device
You can also use it to create a single-color version for different branding needs.
 Try the free logo color changer .
 It's a small tool, but hopefully it saves someone a few minutes of work. Feedback and suggestions are welcome!

October 19, 2026
 How do you know which mockups actually fit your brand?
We’ve been building LogoAI’s mockup generator, and one question kept coming up: after someone uploads a logo, how do we help them decide what to put it on?
 The page starts with familiar options—business cards, T-shirts, iPhones, and more. If you already know what you want, you can pick a scene and generate it right away.
 But sometimes you’re not sure which mockups make sense for your brand. After you upload a logo, the AI analyzes the brand’s colors and industry, then suggests scenes that might be a better fit. For Luma Coffee, for example, it suggests coffee bags, boxes, tins, espresso packs, sampler packs, and brand video mockups.
 The goal is to make both paths easy: start with a familiar template, or click a brand-specific recommendation if you need ideas. You don’t have to come up with a prompt or know every possible scene in advance.
 That’s the part I find interesting: AI can help people decide what to create, as well as create it.
 Would brand-based recommendations be useful in a tool like this? What would you want it to suggest for your own product?

October 9, 2026
 Animating a logo is easy. Keeping it recognizable is the hard part.
Most founders spend a lot of time getting their logo right. But when it's time to create a product demo, launch video, or social media post, there's another challenge: making that logo move.
 And it's not always as simple as it sounds.
 With general AI video tools, you might get a visually impressive animation, but the logo itself can change. Letters get distorted, proportions shift, or the final result looks slightly different from the original design.
 That's not ideal when you've already spent time building a recognizable brand identity.
 We've been exploring this problem at LogoAI.
 Our AI logo animation tool lets you upload an existing logo and experiment with different motion styles, from subtle light effects to more expressive particle and 3D animations.
 The goal isn't to redesign the logo. It's to give an existing brand identity a new way to show up in videos and digital content.
 We're still exploring how to balance creative animation with visual consistency, especially when using more complex AI effects.
 I'm curious how other founders approach this.
 Would you actually use an animated logo for your product? If so, where would it be most useful — your landing page, product demo, launch video, or social media?

October 8, 2026
 Is a Logo Enough to Get Your Startup Branding Started?
When you're building an MVP or launching a side project, branding usually isn't your top priority.
 You create a logo, put it on your landing page, and move on.
 But then you need a social media post, a pitch deck, or a new website section. Suddenly, you're making the same design decisions again: which colors, which fonts, and what actually looks consistent with your logo?
 I've been thinking about this while working on LogoAI.
 Maybe early-stage founders don't need a complete brand identity system. They just need a simple set of guidelines they can actually use.
 An existing logo can already provide a starting point:
 Colors that can become a consistent palette
Typography that complements the logo
Basic rules for logo placement and spacing
A visual direction for websites and marketing materials
AI can help turn these into a first draft of brand guidelines, even if all you have is a logo.
 Of course, a logo can't tell AI who your customers are or what your business stands for. Those decisions still need to come from you.
 That's the idea behind our Brand Guideline tool. You can start with an existing logo and generate an editable guide with colors, typography, logo usage, and brand personality suggestions.
 https://www.logoai.com/brand/guideline
 Curious how other founders approach this: do you create basic brand guidelines before launching, or only after your product starts getting traction?

September 29, 2026
 Not everyone starts a logo from a blank prompt
One thing we’ve learned while building LogoAI: people rarely start from exactly the same place.
 Some have nothing but a brand name.
 Others already have a photo, a rough sketch, an old logo they don’t want to throw away, or even a signature they want to turn into a personal brand.
 That changed how we think about AI logo creation.
 Instead of asking everyone to describe the perfect logo in a long prompt, we’ve been building around different starting points:
 Brand name → explore completely new logo directions
Image or sketch → turn an existing visual idea into a logo
Existing logo → refresh or refine what’s already there
Signature → create a more personal identity
The interesting part for us is that the input changes the role of AI.
 Sometimes AI is generating from scratch. Sometimes it’s interpreting an idea. Sometimes it’s helping preserve what already works while changing what doesn’t.
 We put together a longer breakdown of these four approaches here:
 4 Ways to Create a Logo with AI
 Curious how other founders approached this — when you created your logo, what did you actually start with: a name, a reference image, an existing design, or just a prompt?
