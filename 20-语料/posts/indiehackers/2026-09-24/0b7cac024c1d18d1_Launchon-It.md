 The ultimate guide to AI search optimization: how to get your SaaS cited by ChatGPT, Claude, and Perplexity
If you have looked at your organic traffic channels over the last few months, you probably noticed a massive shift. More and more potential software buyers are skipping traditional Google search results pages entirely and instead asking conversational AI tools like ChatGPT, Claude, and Perplexity for direct recommendations.
 When a user asks an AI tool for the best analytics software or the top productivity tool, the model does not give them ten blue links. It outputs a curated shortlist of three to five brands. If your startup is on that list, you capture high-converting intent. If you are not, you are completely invisible.
 This fundamental shift has given rise to new optimization frameworks: AEO (Answer Engine Optimization) and GEO (Generative Engine Optimization).
 Unlike traditional SEO, which focuses purely on keyword density and search rankings, AI search optimization is built around machine citation authority . Large Language Models do not just rank pages; they synthesize information from trusted third-party networks, structured directories, and entity mentions, then extract those sources directly into their answers.
 What actually moves the needle for AI citations?
 1. Structured Entity Clarity: LLMs rely heavily on structured data, clean directory ecosystems, and consistent brand mentions across platforms like GitHub, Indie Hackers, and specialized curation boards to understand what your software actually does.
 2. Contextual Third-Party Proof: AI models validate your software by looking at how often you are cited alongside competitors in comparisons, alternative lists, and developer docs.
 3. Answer-First Formatting: Pages structured with concise, direct explanations immediately following headings perform significantly better because scrapers and AI retrieval agents can extract them without friction.
 While building tools like LaunchOnIt and monitoring brand footprints, it became obvious that distribution is no longer just about human clicks. It is about planting the digital footprints that AI agents look for when mapping out software categories.
 How are you adapting your distribution strategy for generative search engines? Have you noticed any referral traffic coming directly from AI assistants yet?

 The hidden cost of building features that users never actually touch
As developers and solo founders, our default setting when we hit a wall or want to impress users is always to write more code. We build extra settings, complicated dashboards, and automated toggles because it feels like we are being productive.
 I caught myself doing this recently while looking at the backend code for LaunchOnIt . I had built out a bunch of conditional UI flows and extra options that looked cool on paper, but when I checked the analytics, practically zero users were clicking them. They just wanted to submit their product, get their 7-day runway, and move on.
 So I did what hurts every developer's soul: I deleted the code. Whole blocks of it.
 The moment I stripped away the clutter and made the core submission flow dead simple, everything improved. The page loaded faster, support questions dropped to almost zero, and the conversion rate ticked up.
 We forget that every extra feature we add is not just code we have to maintain, it is extra cognitive load we are forcing onto our users.
 How often do you audit your own app and delete features? Or are you guilty of hoarding code just in case someone might want it someday?
