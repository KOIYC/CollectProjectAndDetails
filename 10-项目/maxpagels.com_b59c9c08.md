Give it a table of data, and it drafts a causal graph of what is likely to cause what.  🤗 maxpagels/axiom-base  Model on Hugging Face Model card Weights  3.8M parameters · fp32 safetensors · 15 MB Contents  Introduction  Causal graphs, briefly  Why discovery is hard  What to do with the output  Running Axiom  Using intervention data  Limitations  Benchmarks Introduction  Drafting a causal graph, a representation of what is thought to cause what, is often the first step in causal inference. This process is known as causal discovery .  Discovery is usually done manually, through a blend of stakeholder interviews and domain expertise. Ideally, the result is a correct directed graph of the causal process of interest. In practice it will never be perfect, but even an imperfect graph is valuable. A partially correct graph still encodes a great deal of knowledge: which variables are plausibly related, which are almost certainly not, and the direction of influence for at least some relationships.  Automated causal discovery aims to speed up this traditionally manual process by inferring such partial graphs directly from data.…
