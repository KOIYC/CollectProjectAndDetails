## 摘要正文

After a few weeks of slowly posting on Reddit, my little side project finally crossed **50 registered users** today.  It’s called [Freshers.dev](http://Freshers.dev), a satirical browser game where your public GitHub profile becomes a fictional software engineer, and you play through a chaotic tech career filled with startups, layoffs, AI hype, promotions, burnout, and questionable life choices.  Some things that surprised me:  * Reddit has been by far my best acquisition channel. * Mobile traffic was much higher than I expected (\~65%). * People spent more time than I expected looking at the leaderboard. * Building is much easier than getting people to care enough to come back.  I’m deliberately taking the slow route instead of doing a big launch. Every week I ship a few improvements, post in one or two communities, collect feedback, and repeat.  For those of you who’ve built products before:  What changed between your first 50 users and your first 500?  Was it:  * A product improvement? * A distribution channel? * A feature that suddenly made people come back? * Or just consistency over time?
