## 摘要正文

Hello HN, I’m Daniel and I run ZoomHub, a small deep zoom image service.Most online catalogs look the same: There’s a grid of thumbnails, limited to about 20–50 items, filters that refresh the page when activated, and pagination buttons to go to page 22 of 128. It has always bothered me that you can’t see the whole collection and every action makes you wait and lose context.I built Brick Atlas to show it doesn’t have to be that way: You can browse 1000+ LEGO sets by zooming in, selecting a set, seeing different angles (at full resolution) and in the sidebar, the set’s piece count, price, etc. You can read the whole instructions (on some sets) without ever seeing a page refresh or losing your place. On a slow connection, the image simply gradually becomes sharper. Once you are done, you can zoom out and see all sets again. Apply a price filter to find gift ideas under $50 or switch to Real size layout to see how big the sets are in real life compared to each other. Toggle Box vs Built to switch between the retail box and the assembled set. For collectors, I added the scatter view to plot sets by piece count vs price so you can find the best value sets.
